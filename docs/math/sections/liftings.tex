\section{Liftings and piecewise affine rules}
\label{sec:liftings}

\subsection{The framework}

Following \citet[Section~3]{GWK2015}, a \emph{lifting} is a nonlinear map $\Lift : \R^k \to \R^{\lk}$, $\lxi = \Lift(\xi)$, with a linear \emph{retraction} $\Retr : \R^{\lk} \to \R^k$ such that
\[
\Lift(\xi)_1 = 1, \qquad \Retr\,\Lift(\xi) = \xi,
\]
and whose components are linearly independent almost surely (axioms (A1)--(A4) of \citet{GWK2015}).
In the multistage case, \citet[axiom (A5)]{GWK2015} also ask that $\Lift$ and $\Retr$ act stage by stage.
A rule that is linear in the lifted vector, $x(\xi) = \lX\Lift(\xi)$, is nonlinear in $\xi$:
the components of $\Lift$ are basis functions.
Since the data of \eqref{eq:P} are linear in $\xi = \Retr\lxi$, the problem can be written in the lifted variable,
with the law of $\lxi = \Lift(\xi)$, the support $\Lift(\Xis)$, and the data
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lifted-data}
B \leftarrow B\Retr, \qquad C \leftarrow C\Retr, \qquad W \leftarrow W\Retr_\eta, \qquad d \leftarrow \Retr_\eta^\T d, \qquad Q \leftarrow \Retr_\eta^\T Q \Retr_\eta,
\end{equation}
with $P$ and $f$ unchanged, where $\Retr_\eta$ is the block of $\Retr$ that maps the lifted uncertainties to $\eta$.
(In the package $\Retr = \blockdiag(1, \Retr_\eta)$, see below.)
This lifted problem is equivalent to \eqref{eq:P}: feasible policies correspond one to one, with the same objective \citep[Propositions~3.4 and~6.3]{GWK2015}.
Its primal approximation restricts the policies of \eqref{eq:P} to the rules $\lX\Lift(\xi)$,
and its dual approximation restricts the dual multipliers to linear combinations of the components of $\Lift$ \citep[Proposition~3.6]{GWK2015};
so they bound $\val\eqref{eq:P}$ from both sides, at least as tightly as the linear rules do \citep[Theorem~3.8]{GWK2015}.

To build them, the approximations need a description of the convex hull of the lifted support $\Lift(\Xis)$, which is not convex,
or a polyhedron $\lXi \supseteq \conv\Lift(\Xis)$ \citep[conditions (S4) and its relaxation]{GWK2015}.
With an outer approximation, the primal approximation remains a feasible policy and the dual approximation a valid bound,
possibly looser \citep[Corollary~2.2]{GWK2015}.
For the lifted problem, \cref{ass:support} asks that $\lXi$ contain the support of the lifted law, which holds when $\lXi \supseteq \Lift(\Xis)$,
and that $\lM = \E[\lxi\lxi^\T] \succ 0$, which is the axiom (A4) of linear independence.

\begin{proposition}[Lifted approximations]
\label{prop:lifted}
Let $\lXi \supseteq \Lift(\Xis)$ be a polyhedron, and let the stage of a lifted coordinate be the largest stage of the coordinates of $\xi$ that it depends on.
Suppose that the retraction writes each coordinate of $\xi$ as a combination of lifted coordinates whose stages are at most its own.
Then the primal approximation of the lifted problem gives the feasible policy $x(\xi) = \lX\Lift(\xi)$ of \eqref{eq:P}, with the same value.
If moreover the lifted law satisfies the conditions of \cref{thm:dual-bound}, the dual approximation of the lifted problem bounds $\val\eqref{eq:P}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For the primal: if the lifted constraints hold on $\lXi$, they hold on $\Lift(\Xis)$, that is, the constraints of \eqref{eq:P} hold for $x(\xi) = \lX\Lift(\xi)$ on $\Xis$;
the rule only uses lifted coordinates of stages up to that of the decision, which depend only on observed coordinates;
and the objectives agree by \eqref{eq:lifted-data}, since $\xi = \Retr\lxi$.
For the dual: a feasible policy $x$ of \eqref{eq:P} gives the policy $\tilde x(\lxi) = x(\Retr\lxi)$ of the lifted problem, with the same objective;
by the hypothesis on $\Retr$ it respects the lifted information structure, and \cref{thm:dual-bound} applied to the lifted problem concludes.
\end{proof}

The package states the stage rule of the proposition in general (\code{AbstractLifting}, \file{src/lifting/lifting.jl}).
For liftings of one coordinate, the only ones implemented, it is the axiom (A5) of \citet{GWK2015};
for liftings of linear combinations of several coordinates (a backlog item), it is the history-dependent lifting of \citet[Remark~6.5]{GWK2015},
which is enough for the primal approximation but not, in general, for the dual one.

\subsection{Axis-aligned piecewise linear liftings}
\label{sec:axis-lifting}

The lifting of version 0.4 is the piecewise linear lifting with axial segmentation of \citet[Section~4.1]{GWK2015}, applied to chosen coordinates (\code{AxisPiecewise}).
Let $\eta$ be one coordinate with support $[\underline\eta, \overline\eta]$, and let $\underline\eta < b_1 < \dots < b_n < \overline\eta$ be breakpoints.
Set $v_0 = \underline\eta$, $v_i = b_i$, $v_{n+1} = \overline\eta$ and $\Delta_i = v_i - v_{i-1}$.
The lifting replaces $\eta$ by $n+1$ coordinates
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:axis-lifting}
\leta_1 = \min(\eta, b_1), \qquad
\leta_i = \min\bigl(\max(\eta - b_{i-1}, 0),\ \Delta_i\bigr) \ (2 \le i \le n), \qquad
\leta_{n+1} = \max(\eta - b_n, 0),
\end{equation}
which is \citet[(3)]{GWK2015}, and the retraction is the row of ones, $\eta = \leta_1 + \dots + \leta_{n+1}$ \citep[(4)]{GWK2015}.
The first piece contains the lower end of the support, so the retraction has no constant term and $\Retr = \blockdiag(1, \Retr_\eta)$.
A rule $X_0 + \sum_i X_i\leta_i$ is continuous and piecewise affine in $\eta$, with slope $X_i$ on the $i$-th piece;
conversely, every such function is of this form \citep[Section~4.1]{GWK2015}.

\begin{lemma}[Convex hull of the lifted segment {\citep[Lemma~4.3]{GWK2015}}]
\label{lem:hull}
With $t_1 = (\leta_1 - \underline\eta)/\Delta_1$ and $t_i = \leta_i/\Delta_i$ for $i \ge 2$,
\[
\conv \Lift([\underline\eta, \overline\eta]) = \{\, \leta \in \R^{n+1} : 1 \ge t_1 \ge t_2 \ge \dots \ge t_{n+1} \ge 0 \,\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The lifted segment is the polygonal path through the $n+2$ points
\[
(\underline\eta, 0, \dots, 0),\ (b_1, 0, \dots, 0),\ (b_1, \Delta_2, 0, \dots, 0),\ \dots,\ (b_1, \Delta_2, \dots, \Delta_{n+1}),
\]
which are affinely independent; in the coordinates $t$ they are $0$, $e_1$, $e_1 + e_2$, \dots, $e_1 + \dots + e_{n+1}$,
the vertices of the simplex $\{1 \ge t_1 \ge \dots \ge t_{n+1} \ge 0\}$.
\end{proof}

The package describes this simplex by the box of the lifted law,
$\underline\eta \le \leta_1 \le b_1$ and $0 \le \leta_i \le \Delta_i$, which contains the two end inequalities,
and by the $n$ \emph{hull rows} $t_i \ge t_{i+1}$, scaled by $\Delta_i\Delta_{i+1}$:
\[
\Delta_{i+1}\leta_i - \Delta_i\leta_{i+1} \ge \Delta_2\underline\eta\, [i = 1], \qquad i = 1, \dots, n
\]
(\code{\_hull\_rows}).
\citet[Lemma~4.3]{GWK2015} write the same facets normalized by the $\Delta$'s, without the redundant box rows.
By \cref{prop:description} neither the scaling nor the redundant rows change the dual bound, and they do not change the primal approximation either;
the scaling keeps the coefficients of the order of the data.
The lifted coordinates of one uncertainty form one support block, with $n+1$ columns and $2(n+1) + n$ rows.

When the lifted coordinate is coupled to others by valid rows, the lifted support is described by the lifted valid rows $W\Retr_\eta\leta \ge h$ and the hull rows of each lifted coordinate.
This is the outer approximation of \citet[(10)]{GWK2015} of the convex hull of the lifted support, exact when $\Xis$ is a box \citep[Theorem~4.4]{GWK2015}.

\subsection{The lifted form}

\code{lift(form, liftings)} (\file{src/lifting/lifted_form.jl}) returns a canonical form over the lifted columns,
so that every formulation applies to it unchanged.
It assembles the sparse retraction $\Retr$ (the identity on the columns that are not lifted, a row of ones for each lifted one),
applies \eqref{eq:lifted-data} with sparse products,
replaces the law of each lifted coordinate by its lifted law (whose box replaces the original box),
and adds the hull rows to the valid rows, with the role \code{:hull}.
Each lifted coordinate is in the stage of its uncertainty, and the columns are sorted by stage again, stably,
so the information structure is still made of prefixes.
For now, a lifted uncertainty must be declared alone, with a univariate law and a bounded support.
Breakpoints are given per uncertainty, as a vector or as a number $n$ of equally spaced interior points (\code{BreakPoints}).

\subsection{Moments of the lifted law}
\label{sec:lifted-moments}

The formulations need the mean and the covariance of the lifted coordinates of each uncertainty.
They depend on the law of $\eta$ only through, for each piece $(v_{i-1}, v_i]$, its probability and the first two moments of $\eta - v_{i-1}$ on it:
\[
p_i = \E[\one_i],
\qquad
m_i = \E\bigl[(\eta - v_{i-1})\,\one_i\bigr],
\qquad
s_i = \E\bigl[(\eta - v_{i-1})^2\,\one_i\bigr],
\]
where $\one_i \defeq \ind{v_{i-1} < \eta \le v_i}$ is the indicator of the $i$-th piece.

\begin{proposition}[Lifted moments]
\label{prop:lifted-moments}
Let $\upsilon_1 = \leta_1 - \underline\eta$, $\upsilon_i = \leta_i$ for $i \ge 2$, and $\theta_i = \sum_{j > i} p_j$, the probability of the pieces after $i$.
Then $\E[\leta] = \E[\upsilon] + \underline\eta e_1$, $\Cov(\leta) = \Cov(\upsilon)$, and
\[
\E[\upsilon_i] = m_i + \Delta_i\theta_i, \qquad
\E[\upsilon_i^2] = s_i + \Delta_i^2\theta_i, \qquad
\E[\upsilon_i\upsilon_j] = \Delta_i\,\E[\upsilon_j] \quad (i < j).
\]
\end{proposition}

The proof (\cref{app:moments}) rests on one observation: $\upsilon_j > 0$ implies $\eta > v_{j-1} \ge v_i$, hence $\upsilon_i = \Delta_i$, for $i < j$.
For a uniform law the piece moments are polynomials in the $\Delta$'s, and the lifted moments are in closed form (\cref{app:moments});
for other continuous laws the package integrates the density on each piece by adaptive Gauss--Kronrod quadrature (QuadGK, relative tolerance $10^{-12}$);
for discrete laws with a finite support it sums over the atoms.
These moments are computed once, when the lifted law is built (\code{LiftedDistribution}, \file{src/probability/distributions/lifted_distribution.jl}).
For a continuous law with positive probability on every piece, $\Cov(\leta) \succ 0$ (the components of the lifting are nonconstant on disjoint pieces, as in the proof of \citet[Proposition~4.1]{GWK2015});
for a discrete law, a piece with fewer than two atoms makes it singular, and \cref{ass:support} fails for the lifted problem.

\subsection{Rules, evaluation and refinement}

The rule matrix of a lifted problem is in lifted coordinates, $x(\xi) = \lX\Lift(\xi)$:
\code{evaluate} and \code{simulate} lift each outcome before applying it,
\code{decision\_rule} shows, for each lifted uncertainty, its breakpoints and the slopes on its pieces,
and \code{rule\_matrix} returns $\lX$ with columns labelled by uncertainty and piece.

Adding breakpoints refines the lifting:
the lifting with breakpoints $B' \supseteq B$ is the composition of the lifting with breakpoints $B$ and of axis liftings of its pieces,
so the rules of the coarse lifting are rules of the fine one.
When the hull descriptions are exact, \citet[Remark~3.5 and Theorem~3.8]{GWK2015} then give monotone bounds:
the primal value improves and the dual bound tightens as breakpoints are added.
The test suite checks this on nested breakpoints (\cref{ex:newsvendor});
note that the equally spaced breakpoints of $n$ and $n+1$ points are not nested, and need not improve.
The primal rule also gives a warm start of the finer problem: the rule $\lX$ of the coarse lifting is the rule $\lX\Retr'$ of the fine one, where $\Retr'$ maps the fine pieces to the coarse ones.
\citet[Theorem~4.4]{GWK2015} show that the sizes stay polynomial in the number of breakpoints;
\cref{sec:computation} reports what that means in practice.
