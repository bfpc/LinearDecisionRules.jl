\section{Liftings and piecewise affine rules}
\label{sec:liftings}

\subsection{The framework}

Following \citet[Section~3]{GWK2015}, a \emph{lifting} is a nonlinear map $\Lift : \R^k \to \R^{\lk}$, $\lxi = \Lift(\xi)$, with a linear \emph{retraction} $\Retr : \R^{\lk} \to \R^k$ such that
\[
\Lift(\xi)_1 = 1, \qquad \Retr\,\Lift(\xi) = \xi,
\]
and whose components are linearly independent (axioms (A1)--(A4) of \citet{GWK2015}).
In the multistage case, \citet[axiom (A5)]{GWK2015} also ask that $\Lift$ and $\Retr$ act stage by stage.
A rule that is linear in the lifted vector, $x(\xi) = \lX\Lift(\xi)$, is nonlinear in $\xi$:
the components of $\Lift$ are basis functions.

Since the data of \eqref{eq:SP} are linear in $\xi = \Retr\lxi$, the problem can be written in the lifted variable,
with the law of $\lxi = \Lift(\xi)$, the support $\Lift(\Xis)$, and the data
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lifted-data}
B \leftarrow B\Retr, \qquad C \leftarrow C\Retr, \qquad W \leftarrow W\Retr_\eta, \qquad d \leftarrow \Retr_\eta^\T d, \qquad Q \leftarrow \Retr_\eta^\T Q \Retr_\eta,
\end{equation}
with $P$ and $f$ unchanged, where $\Retr_\eta$ is the block of $\Retr$ that maps the lifted uncertainties to $\eta$.
(In the package $\Retr = \blockdiag(1, \Retr_\eta)$, see below.)
The lifted problem with decisions $x(\lxi)$ is equivalent to \eqref{eq:SP}:
feasible policies correspond one to one, with the same objective \citep[Propositions~3.4 and~6.3]{GWK2015}.
Its primal approximation restricts the policies of \eqref{eq:SP} to rules $\lX\Lift(\xi)$,
and its dual approximation restricts the dual multipliers to linear combinations of the components of $\Lift$ \citep[Proposition~3.6]{GWK2015};
so they bound $\val\eqref{eq:SP}$ from both sides, at least as tightly as the linear rules do \citep[Theorem~3.8]{GWK2015}.

To build the lifted approximations,
one needs a description of the lifted support $\Lift(\Xis)$,
or its convex hull,
or at least a convenient description of a convex set
$\lXi \supseteq \conv\Lift(\Xis)$ \citep[conditions (S4) and its relaxation]{GWK2015}.
With an outer approximation, the primal approximation remains a feasible policy,
and the dual approximation a valid bound,
possibly looser \citep[Corollary~2.2]{GWK2015}.
For the lifted problem, \cref{ass:support} asks that $\lXi$ contain the support of the lifted law, which holds when $\lXi \supseteq \Lift(\Xis)$,
and that $\lM = \E[\lxi\lxi^\T] \succ 0$, which is the axiom (A4) of linear independence.

\begin{proposition}[Lifted approximations]
\label{prop:lifted}
Let $\lXi \supseteq \Lift(\Xis)$ be a polyhedron, and let the stage of a lifted coordinate be the largest stage of the coordinates of $\xi$ that it depends on.
Suppose that the retraction writes each coordinate of $\xi$ as a combination of lifted coordinates whose stages are at most its own.
Then the primal approximation of the lifted problem
gives the feasible policy $x(\xi) = \lX\Lift(\xi)$ for \eqref{eq:SP},
with the same objective value.
If moreover the lifted law satisfies the conditions of \cref{thm:dual-bound},
the dual approximation of the lifted problem bounds $\val\eqref{eq:SP}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For the primal: if the lifted constraints hold on $\lXi$, they hold on $\Lift(\Xis)$, that is, the constraints of \eqref{eq:SP} hold for $x(\xi) = \lX\Lift(\xi)$ on $\Xis$;
the rule only uses lifted coordinates of stages up to that of the decision, which depend only on observed coordinates;
and the objectives agree by \eqref{eq:lifted-data}, since $\xi = \Retr\lxi$.
For the dual: a feasible policy $x$ of \eqref{eq:SP} gives the policy $\tilde x(\lxi) = x(\Retr\lxi)$ of the lifted problem, with the same objective;
by the hypothesis on $\Retr$ it respects the lifted information structure, and \cref{thm:dual-bound} applied to the lifted problem concludes.
\end{proof}

The package states the stage rule of the proposition in general (\code{AbstractLifting}, \file{src/lifting/lifting.jl}).
For liftings of one coordinate, it is the axiom (A5) of \citet{GWK2015};
for liftings of linear combinations of several coordinates (\cref{sec:combination-lifting}), it is the history-dependent lifting of \citet[Remark~6.5]{GWK2015},
which is enough for the primal approximation but not, in general, for the dual one.

\subsection{Axis-aligned piecewise linear liftings}
\label{sec:axis-lifting}

In version 0.4, we provide piecewise linear lifting with axial segmentation \citet[Section~4.1]{GWK2015}, applied to chosen coordinates (\code{AxisPiecewise}).
Let $\eta$ be one coordinate with support $[\underline\eta, \overline\eta]$, and let $\underline\eta < b_1 < \dots < b_n < \overline\eta$ be breakpoints.
Set $v_0 = \underline\eta$, $v_i = b_i$, $v_{n+1} = \overline\eta$ and $\Delta_i = v_i - v_{i-1}$.
The lifting replaces $\eta$ by $n+1$ coordinates
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:axis-lifting}
\leta_1 = \min(\eta, b_1), \qquad
\leta_i = \min\bigl(\max(\eta - b_{i-1}, 0),\ \Delta_i\bigr) \ (2 \le i \le n), \qquad
\leta_{n+1} = \max(\eta - b_n, 0),
\end{equation}
which is \citet[(3)]{GWK2015}, and the retraction is the row of ones, $\eta = \leta_1 + \dots + \leta_{n+1}$ \citep[(4)]{GWK2015}.
The first piece contains the lower end of the support, so the retraction has no constant term and $\Retr = \blockdiag(1, \Retr_\eta)$
(\cref{rem:shifted-lifting} discusses the alternative).
A rule $X_0 + \sum_i X_i\leta_i$ is continuous and piecewise affine in $\eta$, with slope $X_i$ on the $i$-th piece;
conversely, every such function is of this form \citep[Section~4.1]{GWK2015}.

\begin{remark}[Pieces that start at zero]
\label{rem:shifted-lifting}
One could also start every piece at zero:
$\leta_1 = \min(\eta, b_1) - \underline\eta$, the other pieces unchanged, and $\eta = \underline\eta + \leta_1 + \dots + \leta_{n+1}$.
Every piece then lies in $[0, \Delta_i]$, $t_i = \leta_i/\Delta_i$ for every $i$ in \cref{lem:hull},
and the hull rows below lose their right-hand side $\Delta_2\underline\eta\,[i = 1]$.

The approximations do not change.
The change of coordinates that subtracts $\underline\eta\,\lxi_1$ from the first piece is a map $G$ of \cref{prop:affine-invariance}, with $s = \one$:
it keeps $\lxi_1 = 1$ and the stage of every coordinate, and maps the rules to rules, the lifted support to its image, and $\lM$ to $G^{-1}\lM G^{-\T}$,
so the values of the approximations are the same.
The rescaling of the inner problems (\cref{sec:rescaling}) is such a map too, applied after the lifting, and it leaves the convention below unchanged for the user.

The retraction is still linear in $\lxi$, but it is no longer block diagonal: the row of $\eta$ is $(\underline\eta, 1, \dots, 1)$.
Write $\eta = v + \Retr_\eta\leta$, where $v$ holds the lower ends of the lifted coordinates and zeros elsewhere.
In the package, this would change:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{The data written over $\eta$ alone.}
$B \leftarrow B\Retr$ and $C \leftarrow C\Retr$ are unchanged, since they act on $\xi$,
but in \eqref{eq:lifted-data} the support gets $h \leftarrow h - Wv$,
and the objective $d \leftarrow \Retr_\eta^\T(d + 2Qv)$ and $f \leftarrow f + d^\T v + v^\T Q v$.
\item \emph{The parameters.}
The first column of $B\Retr$ is that of $B$ plus the columns of $B$ on $\eta$ times $v$,
so the constants of \code{ParameterTerms} must absorb this term;
today \code{lift} keeps them as they are, because the first column of $\Retr$ is $\eone$.
\item \emph{The lifting.}
$\Lift$ depends on the lower end of the support, which \code{AxisPiecewise} does not hold (it holds only the breakpoints),
and \code{retraction} would no longer be a row of ones.
Both are public, as is \code{LiftingMap}, so the change would be breaking for those who use them.
\item \emph{The lifted law.}
The mean of the first piece shifts by $\underline\eta$, its covariance is unchanged, and its box becomes $[0, \Delta_1]$.
\item \emph{The reported rules.}
The constant of a lifted rule absorbs $\underline\eta$ times its slope on the first piece,
so \code{rule\_coefficient} and \code{rule\_matrix} would report another constant;
the slopes, and the rule as a function of $\eta$ (\code{decision\_rule}), are unchanged.
\item \emph{The tests} of the lifted coordinates, of the hull rows and of the lifted moments.
\end{itemize}
The gain is in the notation, the cost in the code and in the public API;
the package keeps the convention of \citet[(3)--(4)]{GWK2015}, whose retraction is a row of ones.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Convex hull of the lifted segment {\citep[Lemma~4.3]{GWK2015}}]
\label{lem:hull}
With $t_1 = (\leta_1 - \underline\eta)/\Delta_1$ and $t_i = \leta_i/\Delta_i$ for $i \ge 2$,
\[
\conv \Lift([\underline\eta, \overline\eta]) = \{\, \leta \in \R^{n+1} : 1 \ge t_1 \ge t_2 \ge \dots \ge t_{n+1} \ge 0 \,\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The lifted segment is the polygonal path through the $n+2$ points
\[
(\underline\eta, 0, \dots, 0),\ (b_1, 0, \dots, 0),\ (b_1, \Delta_2, 0, \dots, 0),\ \dots,\ (b_1, \Delta_2, \dots, \Delta_{n+1}),
\]
which are affinely independent; in the coordinates $t$ they are $0$, $e_1$, $e_1 + e_2$, \dots, $e_1 + \dots + e_{n+1}$,
the vertices of the simplex $\{1 \ge t_1 \ge \dots \ge t_{n+1} \ge 0\}$.
\end{proof}

The package describes this simplex by its $n+2$ facets:
the two bounds $\leta_1 \le b_1$ and $\leta_{n+1} \ge 0$ of the box of the lifted law, that is $t_1 \le 1$ and $t_{n+1} \ge 0$,
and the $n$ \emph{hull rows} $t_i \ge t_{i+1}$, scaled by $\Delta_i\Delta_{i+1}$:
\[
\Delta_{i+1}\leta_i - \Delta_i\leta_{i+1} \ge \Delta_2\underline\eta\, [i = 1], \qquad i = 1, \dots, n
\]
(\code{\_hull\_rows}).
The other bounds of the box, $\leta_1 \ge \underline\eta$ and $0 \le \leta_i \le \Delta_i$ otherwise, follow from the chain $1 \ge t_1 \ge \dots \ge t_{n+1} \ge 0$:
they remain bounds of the lifted law, for its draws and the checks of scenarios.
\citet[Lemma~4.3]{GWK2015} write the same facets normalized by the $\Delta$'s.
By \cref{prop:description} neither the scaling nor redundant rows change the dual bound, and they do not change the primal approximation either;
the scaling avoids dividing by the lengths of the pieces.
The rescaling of \cref{sec:rescaling} turns these rows into $\zeta_i - \zeta_{i+1} \ge 0$.
The lifted coordinates of one uncertainty form one support block, with $n+1$ columns and $n+2$ rows,
or $3n+2$ with the $2n$ redundant bounds.
Each formulation chooses (\code{lifted\_box} of \code{PrimalLDR} and \code{DualLDR}, in \code{support\_blocks}):
the primal approximation leaves the redundant bounds out, and the dual approximation keeps them, for the speed of the solver (\cref{sec:faster}).

When the lifted coordinate is coupled to others by valid rows, the lifted support is described by the lifted valid rows $W\Retr_\eta\leta \ge h$ and the rows above of each lifted coordinate.
This is the outer approximation of \citet[(10)]{GWK2015} of the convex hull of the lifted support, exact when $\Xis$ is a box \citep[Theorem~4.4]{GWK2015}.

\subsection{The lifted form}

\code{lift(form, liftings)} (\file{src/lifting/lifted_form.jl}) returns a canonical form over the lifted columns,
so that every formulation applies to it unchanged.
It assembles the sparse retraction $\Retr$ (the identity on the columns that are not lifted, a row of ones for each lifted one),
applies \eqref{eq:lifted-data} with sparse products,
replaces the law of each lifted coordinate by its lifted law (whose box replaces the original box),
and adds the hull rows to the valid rows, with the role \code{:hull}.
Each lifted coordinate is in the stage of its uncertainty, and the columns are sorted by stage again, stably,
so the information structure is still made of prefixes.
For now, a lifted uncertainty must be declared alone, with a univariate law and a bounded support.
Breakpoints are given per uncertainty, as a vector or as a number $n$ of equally spaced interior points (\code{BreakPoints}).

\subsection{Moments of the lifted law}
\label{sec:lifted-moments}

The formulations need the mean and the covariance of the lifted coordinates of each uncertainty.
They depend on the law of $\eta$ only through, for each piece $(v_{i-1}, v_i]$, its probability and the first two moments of $\eta - v_{i-1}$ on it:
\[
p_i = \E[\one_i],
\qquad
m_i = \E\bigl[(\eta - v_{i-1})\,\one_i\bigr],
\qquad
s_i = \E\bigl[(\eta - v_{i-1})^2\,\one_i\bigr],
\]
where $\one_i \defeq \ind{v_{i-1} < \eta \le v_i}$ is the indicator of the $i$-th piece.

\begin{proposition}[Lifted moments]
\label{prop:lifted-moments}
Let $\upsilon_1 = \leta_1 - \underline\eta$, $\upsilon_i = \leta_i$ for $i \ge 2$, and $\theta_i = \sum_{j > i} p_j$, the probability of the pieces after $i$.
Then $\E[\leta] = \E[\upsilon] + \underline\eta e_1$, $\Cov(\leta) = \Cov(\upsilon)$, and
\[
\E[\upsilon_i] = m_i + \Delta_i\theta_i, \qquad
\E[\upsilon_i^2] = s_i + \Delta_i^2\theta_i, \qquad
\E[\upsilon_i\upsilon_j] = \Delta_i\,\E[\upsilon_j] \quad (i < j).
\]
\end{proposition}

The proof (\cref{app:moments}) rests on one observation: $\upsilon_j > 0$ implies $\eta > v_{j-1} \ge v_i$, hence $\upsilon_i = \Delta_i$, for $i < j$.
For a uniform law the piece moments are polynomials in the $\Delta$'s, and the lifted moments are in closed form (\cref{app:moments});
for other continuous laws the package integrates the density on each piece by adaptive Gauss--Kronrod quadrature (QuadGK, relative tolerance $10^{-12}$);
for discrete laws with a finite support it sums over the atoms.
These moments are computed once, when the lifted law is built (\code{LiftedDistribution}, \file{src/probability/distributions/lifted_distribution.jl}).
For a continuous law with positive probability on every piece, $\Cov(\leta) \succ 0$ (the components of the lifting are nonconstant on disjoint pieces, as in the proof of \citet[Proposition~4.1]{GWK2015});
for a discrete law, $\Cov(\leta)$ is singular exactly when a nonzero continuous piecewise linear function with the same breakpoints vanishes at every atom,
since the affine functions $a + c^\T \leta$ of the lifted vector are, as functions of $\eta$, all such functions.
A law with no more atoms than pieces is therefore always singular, and then \cref{ass:support} fails for the lifted problem;
two atoms in one piece and one atom in the interior of every other piece suffice for $\Cov(\leta) \succ 0$,
because the function then vanishes on that piece and, piece after piece, on each neighbour, which it meets at a breakpoint.

\subsection{Rules, evaluation and refinement}

The rule matrix of a lifted problem is in lifted coordinates, $x(\xi) = \lX\Lift(\xi)$:
\code{evaluate} and \code{simulate} lift each outcome before applying it,
\code{decision\_rule} shows, for each lifted uncertainty, its breakpoints and the slopes on its pieces,
and \code{rule\_matrix} returns $\lX$ with columns labelled by uncertainty and piece.

Adding breakpoints refines the lifting:
the lifting with breakpoints $B' \supseteq B$ is the composition of the lifting with breakpoints $B$ and of axis liftings of its pieces,
so the rules of the coarse lifting are rules of the fine one.
When the hull descriptions are exact, \citet[Remark~3.5 and Theorem~3.8]{GWK2015} then give monotone bounds:
the primal value improves and the dual bound tightens as breakpoints are added.
The test suite checks this on nested breakpoints (\cref{ex:newsvendor});
note that the equally spaced breakpoints of $n$ and $n+1$ points are not nested, and need not improve.
The primal rule also gives a warm start of the finer problem: the rule $\lX$ of the coarse lifting is the rule $\lX\Retr'$ of the fine one, where $\Retr'$ maps the fine pieces to the coarse ones.
\citet[Theorem~4.4]{GWK2015} show that the sizes stay polynomial in the number of breakpoints;
\cref{sec:computation} reports what that means in practice.

\subsection{Liftings of combinations of uncertainties (experimental)}
\label{sec:combination-lifting}

An axis lifting makes a rule piecewise affine in one uncertainty.
Often the decisions react to a combination of uncertainties instead:
the total inflow of a stage, the inflows of one reservoir over two stages, the demand of two periods.
The package puts breakpoints on such a combination, as an experimental feature
(\code{CombinationPiecewise}, \file{src/lifting/combination_piecewise.jl}, set with \code{Experimental.set\_breakpoints}).

Let $J$ be a set of uncertainties, $a \in \R^J$ nonzero, $c \in \R$, and $s = c + a^\T\eta_J$.
Let $[s_{\min}, s_{\max}]$ be the range of $s$ on the support
(on the atoms of the laws with finitely many, on the box of the others),
let $s_{\min} < b_1 < \dots < b_n < s_{\max}$ be breakpoints,
and let $v_i$ and $\Delta_i$ be as in \cref{sec:axis-lifting}, for $s$.
The lifting appends the pieces of $s$ after the first to the uncertainties, which it keeps:
\[
\Lift(\xi) = \bigl(\xi,\ p_2(s), \dots, p_{n+1}(s)\bigr),
\]
with
\[
p_k = \min\bigl(\max(s - b_{k-1}, 0),\ \Delta_k\bigr) \ (2 \le k \le n), \qquad
p_{n+1} = \max(s - b_n, 0).
\]
The first piece, $\min(s, b_1) = s - p_2 - \dots - p_{n+1}$, is affine in the lifted vector,
so it is not a coordinate: the components of $\Lift$ would not be linearly independent.
The retraction keeps $\xi$ and drops the pieces, $\Retr = [\eye\ \ 0]$,
and each piece is in the largest stage of the uncertainties of $J$.
A rule linear in $\Lift(\xi)$ is
\[
x(\xi) = X_0 + X_\eta\,\eta + \textstyle\sum_{k=2}^{n+1} X_k\,p_k(s),
\]
an affine function of $\eta$ plus a continuous piecewise affine function of $s$, with breakpoints $b_1, \dots, b_n$.
With one input ($s = \eta_j$), the lifted vector $(\eta, p_2, \dots, p_{n+1})$ is the image of the axis lifting of $\eta_j$
by an invertible linear map that keeps $\lxi_1$ and the stages ($\eta_j = \leta_1 + \dots + \leta_{n+1}$, $p_k = \leta_k$),
so by the argument of \cref{rem:shifted-lifting} both approximations are those of \code{AxisPiecewise};
the test suite checks it.

\paragraph{The lifted support.}
Following \citet{GWK2015}, for whom the exact convex hull is intractable for a general segmentation, the package uses an outer approximation:
\[
\lXi = \bigl\{ (\eta, p) : \eta \in \Xis,\ (s - p_2 - \dots - p_{n+1},\ p_2, \dots, p_{n+1}) \in S \bigr\},
\]
where $S$ is the simplex of \cref{lem:hull} for $s$ on $[s_{\min}, s_{\max}]$.
It contains $\Lift(\Xis)$, since $s \in [s_{\min}, s_{\max}]$ on $\Xis$, so the primal rule is feasible and the dual bound valid (\cref{prop:lifted}).
Its rows are those of $\Xis$ and the facets of $S$ written with the first piece above:
\[
\Delta_2\bigl(s - \textstyle\sum_k p_k - s_{\min}\bigr) - \Delta_1 p_2 \ge 0, \qquad
\Delta_{k+1}p_k - \Delta_k p_{k+1} \ge 0 \ (2 \le k \le n), \qquad
b_1 - s + \textstyle\sum_k p_k \ge 0,
\]
and the bound $p_{n+1} \ge 0$ (\code{\_hull\_rows}).
This bound is a facet of $S$, so the box of the lifted law keeps it even when \code{lifted\_box} is false;
the other bounds of the pieces are implied, as for the axis lifting.

With one input, $\lXi$ is the description of \cref{sec:axis-lifting}.
With several, it is not the convex hull of $\Lift(\Xis)$: its fibers depend on $\eta$ only through $s$.
For example, let $\eta$ be uniform on $[0, 1]^2$, $s = \eta_1 + \eta_2$, and $b_1 = 1$.
Above the vertex $\eta = (1, 0)$ of $\Xis$, the convex hull has the single point $p_2 = 0$, because a vertex is not a convex combination of other points of $\Xis$;
the fiber of $\lXi$ is $\{p_2 : 1 \ge 1 - p_2 \ge p_2 \ge 0\} = [0, 1/2]$.
A robust constraint of the primal approximation must hold on the whole fiber,
so the pieces help less than they would with the exact hull, and the measurements below suggest that the gap grows with the number of inputs.

\paragraph{The dual approximation.}
When the uncertainties of $J$ are in several stages, the pieces are in the last one,
and their conditional expectation given the earlier stages is a nonlinear function of the observed uncertainties:
condition~\ref{it:lce} of \cref{thm:dual-bound} fails, and the dual approximation is not known to bound the problem.
The primal approximation needs no such condition: this is the history-dependent lifting of \citet[Remark~6.5]{GWK2015}.
So \code{DualLDR} refuses these combinations before any solve.
A combination within one stage $t$ keeps the dual bound, unless a law of its inputs has a component in a stage before $t$.
Its pieces, in stage $t$, couple the laws of its inputs into one law (below), which may span several stages, since a law of an input may have components in other stages than $t$.
From stage $t$ on, the pieces are functions of the observed uncertainties, so they add nothing to condition~\ref{it:lce}, which is then that of each law of the inputs (\cref{rem:lce}).
Before stage $t$, the conditional expectation of a piece given the earlier components of the laws of its inputs is a nonlinear function of these components, unless the laws have none: it is then a constant.
So the package certifies the bound when every combination is within one stage, no law of its inputs has a component in an earlier stage (the components of a product are separate laws), and each of these laws has linear conditional expectations;
with a component in an earlier stage, condition~\ref{it:lce} is not known to hold, and the bound is not certified
(\code{\_linear\_conditional\_expectations} of \code{\_CombinationLaw}).

\paragraph{The lifted law and its moments.}
A combination couples the laws of its inputs, so these laws, with the pieces of every combination over them, form one law
(\code{\_CombinationLaw}, \file{src/probability/distributions/combination_law.jl});
combinations that share an input share this law, which is one support block and one covariance block.
The laws must be independent declarations, each discrete with finitely many atoms or continuous and univariate (product laws are split into their components).
The formulations need $\E[p_k]$, $\E[p_k\eta_j]$ and $\E[p_kp_l]$, also between pieces of two combinations that share an input.
The pieces are differences of hinges $(s - b)_+$, so the moments of the pieces depend only on the law of $s$,
$\E[p_k\eta_j]$ on that of $(\eta_j, s - a_j\eta_j)$, and those of two combinations on the laws they share and on the laws of the two rests.
When the laws are discrete with finitely many atoms or uniform, the package computes the moments exactly from the laws of these sums:
the convolution of the discrete terms, and a closed form for the sum of the uniform ones,
with one-dimensional integrals over the law of an input or over the shared laws
(\code{\_SumLaw}, \file{src/probability/distributions/sum_law.jl}).
A combination of five uniform demands then takes a millisecond.
For other continuous laws, and for two combinations whose shared uniform laws have coefficients that are not proportional,
it sums over the joint atoms of the discrete laws involved (at most $10^7$)
and nests adaptive quadratures over the continuous inputs (QuadGK),
split at the values where $s$ crosses a breakpoint, so that the integrand is smooth on each cell:
the cost grows exponentially with the number of continuous inputs, 12~seconds for five uniform demands.
A separate note, \file{docs/math/lifted-moments.tex}, states the hypotheses on the laws and derives both computations.

\paragraph{Measurements.}
\Cref{tab:combinations} gives the primal values of the two benchmark problems of \cref{sec:computation},
with 5 breakpoints per combination for the hydrothermal problem, at the equiprobable quantiles of its law,
and 1 for the inventory, in the middle of its range.
Combinations of two inputs help the most, those of four or eight inputs less (not at all at $T = 3$),
and the cumulative demand of the inventory, its natural state, not at all.
A chain of combinations over consecutive stages merges the laws of all the stages into one support block:
the hydrothermal problem with the inflows of each reservoir over two stages takes 404~s at $T = 5$ (about 50~s of moments and 300~s of solver),
against 1.3~s without breakpoints.
