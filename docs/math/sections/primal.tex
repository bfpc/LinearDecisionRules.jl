\section{The primal approximation}
\label{sec:primal}

\subsection{Robust counterparts}

The primal approximation keeps every inequality \eqref{eq:slack-rule} for all $\xi \in \Xis$.
Each one is a robust linear constraint, and linear programming duality turns it into finitely many linear constraints.
In the form of \citet[Proposition~1]{KWG2011}, with $\Xis = \{\xi : W\xi \ge h\}$ including the rows $\pm\eone^\T\xi \ge \pm1$ that fix $\xi_1 = 1$:
$z^\T\xi \ge 0$ for all $\xi\in\Xis$ if and only if there is $\lambda \ge 0$ with $W^\T\lambda = z$ and $h^\T\lambda \ge 0$.
Applied to every row, this gives the linear program \citet[(2.3)]{KWG2011}, and \citet[(4.2)]{KWG2011} in the multistage case,
with one multiplier per row of $W$ and per inequality: a dense matrix $\Lambda \ge 0$ with $\Lambda W = S$ and $\Lambda h \ge 0$.

The product structure \eqref{eq:product-support} lets us do much better.
Split a slack rule along the blocks, $s(\xi) = s_1 + \sum_b s_b^\T \eta_b$, where $s_b$ is the subvector of $S_r$ on the columns of block $b$.

\begin{proposition}[Block robust counterpart]
\label{prop:block-counterpart}
Let $\Touch$ be a set of support blocks that contains every block $b$ with $s_b \neq 0$.
Then $s(\xi) \ge 0$ for all $\xi \in \Xis$ if and only if there are $\lambda_b \in \R^{q_b}$, $b \in \Touch$, with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:block-counterpart}
\lambda_b \ge 0, \qquad W_b^\T \lambda_b = s_b \quad (b \in \Touch), \qquad s_1 + \sum_{b \in \Touch} h_b^\T \lambda_b \ge 0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The minimum of $s$ over the product $\Xis$ separates:
$\min_{\Xis} s = s_1 + \sum_b \min_{\eta_b \in \Xib{b}} s_b^\T\eta_b$.
A block with $s_b = 0$ contributes $0$, since $\Xib{b}$ is nonempty.
For the others, linear programming duality, valid because $\Xib{b}$ is nonempty, gives
$\min\{ s_b^\T\eta_b : W_b\eta_b \ge h_b\} = \max\{ h_b^\T\lambda_b : W_b^\T\lambda_b = s_b,\ \lambda_b \ge 0\}$,
both sides being $-\infty$ when the dual is infeasible.
So $\min_\Xis s \ge 0$ if and only if multipliers exist as in \eqref{eq:block-counterpart}.
The details, and the link with the dense form, are in \cref{app:primal}.
\end{proof}

The rule $s$ depends on $X$, which is not known when the problem is built, so neither are the blocks with $s_b \neq 0$.
The proposition does not need them:
a block of $\Touch$ with $s_b = 0$ admits $\lambda_b = 0$, which adds nothing to the coupling inequality.
It is enough that $\Touch$ contain every block on which $s_b$ can be nonzero for some $X \in \Rules$,
and the structure of the row gives such a set before $X$ is known (see $\Touch_r$ below).

\paragraph{Conic blocks.}
When block $b$ has second-order cone rows, $\Xib{b} = \{\eta_b : W_b\eta_b - h_b \in \mathcal{K}_b\}$ (\cref{sec:support}),
and \eqref{eq:block-counterpart} holds with $\lambda_b \in \mathcal{K}_b^* = \mathcal{K}_b$ in place of $\lambda_b \ge 0$:
the multipliers of the linear rows are nonnegative, and those of each conic row lie in its second-order cone \citep[Theorem~2.1]{GWK2015}.
Weak conic duality gives the ``if'' direction, so the primal rule is feasible on $\Xis$ in any case.
The ``only if'' direction needs strong duality, which holds when some point of $\Xib{b}$ satisfies its conic rows strictly (Slater's condition),
as the ellipsoid of \cref{sec:confidence-normal} does; without it, the counterpart may be conservative.
With a conic row, the inner problem is a second-order cone program.

For the inequality $r$, the package takes for $\Touch$ the set $\Touch_r$ of the blocks that meet its structural columns $\Jr_r \setminus \{1\}$ (\code{\_touched\_blocks}, \file{src/formulations/primal.jl}).
Since blocks are sorted by their first column, those that meet the observed prefix $2{:}\nvis_r$ are exactly the blocks whose first column is at most $\nvis_r$, a prefix of the list of blocks;
the blocks of the nonzeros of $b_r$ beyond $\nvis_r$ are added to it.
For each $b \in \Touch_r$, the robust counterpart has $q_b$ multipliers and $k_b$ equalities, one per column of the block;
and each inequality has one \emph{coupling} inequality.
The primal approximation solved by the package is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:primal-lp}
\begin{aligned}
\opt_{X \in \Rules,\ \lambda} \quad & \text{objective of \cref{prop:objective}} \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & (a_r^\T X)_j = b_{rj}, \quad j \in \Jr_r, \quad \text{for the equality rows } r,\\
& S_{r1} \ge 0 \quad \text{for the deterministic rows } r \in \Rows,\\
& \lambda_{rb} \ge 0,\ W_b^\T\lambda_{rb} = S_{r,b}\ (b \in \Touch_r),\quad S_{r1} + \sum_{b\in\Touch_r} h_b^\T\lambda_{rb} \ge 0 \quad \text{for the other } r \in \Rows,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $S_r = \sigma_r(b_r^\T - a_r^\T X)$ is an affine expression of $X$, not a variable.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:primal-dense}
Problem \eqref{eq:primal-lp} is equivalent to \eqref{eq:PLDR}, and to the dense linear program of \citet[(4.2)]{KWG2011} for the same support:
the three have the same feasible rules $X$ and the same objective.
\end{corollary}

The dense program is recovered by three changes, each exact (\cref{app:primal}):
the two multipliers of the rows $\pm\eone^\T\xi \ge \pm 1$ only enter through their difference, which equals $s_1$, and are eliminated into the coupling inequality;
the multipliers of the untouched blocks can be set to zero;
and the explicit slack variables of the previous version, with their equalities $AX + S = B$, are substituted by expressions (the option \code{SlackAsExpr} of the previous version, now the only form).
Explicit slacks would add one variable and one equality per inequality and structural column, and save nothing.

A touched block without rows ($q_b = 0$, an unbounded coordinate) leaves the equalities $0 = s_b$:
an inequality cannot depend on an uncertainty that is unbounded in both directions, which is the right answer.

\subsection{Box blocks}

The most common block is a single coordinate $\eta_j$ with a bounded law, $\Xib{b} = [\underline\eta_j, \overline\eta_j]$.
Its rows are $\eta_j \ge \underline\eta_j$ and $-\eta_j \ge -\overline\eta_j$, so \eqref{eq:block-counterpart} reads
$\lambda^+ - \lambda^- = s_j$, $\lambda^\pm \ge 0$, with the contribution $\underline\eta_j\lambda^+ - \overline\eta_j\lambda^-$ to the coupling.
At the optimum it equals $\min(\underline\eta_j s_j, \overline\eta_j s_j) = c_j s_j - \rho_j\abs{s_j}$,
with the center $c_j$ and the radius $\rho_j$ of the interval.
For a row that only touches box blocks, the robust counterpart is thus
\[
s_1 + \sum_j \bigl(c_j s_j - \rho_j \abs{s_j}\bigr) \ge 0,
\]
which a hand-written model expresses with one auxiliary variable $w_j \ge \pm s_j$ per coefficient
(the reference model of \file{benchmark/timing.jl}).
The generic form uses two multipliers and one equality per coefficient instead;
presolve reduces both to the same problem (\cref{sec:computation}).
The package uses the closed form on every block whose rows are only finite bounds of its coordinates (\code{\_add\_box\_counterpart!}, \file{src/formulations/primal.jl}), written with the endpoint $e_j \in \{\underline\eta_j, \overline\eta_j\}$ of smaller magnitude and one auxiliary $w_j \ge 0$:
\[
\min(\underline\eta_j s_j, \overline\eta_j s_j) = \max\bigl\{e_j s_j - (\overline\eta_j - \underline\eta_j) w_j : w_j \ge 0,\ w_j \ge \pm s_j\bigr\},
\]
with the sign $+$ for $e_j = \overline\eta_j$ and $-$ for $e_j = \underline\eta_j$;
this has one variable fewer than the multipliers and as many rows, and one row fewer than $w_j \ge \pm s_j$ with $c_j s_j - \rho_j w_j$ in the coupling.
A constant $s_j$ or a point $\underline\eta_j = \overline\eta_j$ needs no auxiliary, the other blocks of a row keep their multipliers in the same coupling inequality, and \code{PrimalLDR(; box\_closed\_form = false)} uses the multipliers on box blocks too.

\subsection{What the structure saves}

For an inequality $r$, the dense program has $2 + \sum_b q_b$ multipliers and $k$ equalities, whatever the inequality;
the structured one has $\sum_{b\in\Touch_r} q_b$ multipliers and $\sum_{b\in\Touch_r} k_b$ equalities.
In a multistage problem, a stage-$t$ inequality touches only the blocks of the stages up to $t$ (plus the blocks of its own right-hand side),
so on average about half of the blocks with stagewise blocks of equal size;
when the blocks are small, as with independent scalar uncertainties, the saving per block is the whole of the other blocks.
On the $T$-period inventory problem of \file{benchmark/timing.jl}, with one scalar demand per stage, the structured primal has
$4T^2$ multipliers against $8T(T+1)$ for the dense one (\cref{app:sizes});
at $T = 32$, the whole program has 5,744 variables, 2,734 constraints and 13,101 nonzeros,
against 10,096, 5,408 and 21,807 for the dense program on the same canonical form (\file{test/formulations/test_size.jl}).
The closed form on the boxes replaces the $4T^2$ multipliers by $2T^2$ auxiliaries: 3,696 variables, 2,734 constraints and 12,109 nonzeros at $T = 32$.
The gain is smaller when one law couples all the stages:
then every inequality of stage at least 2 touches the single block, and the structured primal is about as large as the dense one.
