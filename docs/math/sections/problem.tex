\section{Problem and notation}
\label{sec:problem}

This section fixes the class of problems that the package accepts,
in the canonical form built by \code{canonicalize} (\file{src/canonical/canonicalize.jl}).

\subsection{Uncertainty, stages and laws}

The uncertain parameters are a random vector $\eta \in \R^m$.
As in \citet{KWG2011}, we prepend a constant and work with
\[
\xi = (\xi_1, \xi_2, \dots, \xi_k) = (1, \eta) \in \R^k, \qquad k = 1 + m,
\]
so that affine functions of $\eta$ are linear functions of $\xi$.
The uncertainties are revealed in stages $1, \dots, T$, with $T \ge 2$:
column $j$ of $\xi$ has a stage $\stage_j$, the constant column being in stage $\stage_1 = 1$.
The columns are sorted by stage, stably with respect to the order of declaration,
so that $\stage_1 \le \stage_2 \le \dots \le \stage_k$.
The history of the observations up to stage $t$ is then a prefix of $\xi$,
\[
\xi_{1:\nvis(t)}, \qquad \nvis(t) \defeq \max\{\, j : \stage_j \le t \,\},
\]
where $\xi_{1:v}$ denotes the first $v$ components of $\xi$.
This is the role of the truncation operators $P_t$ of \citet[Section~4]{KWG2011}, $P_t\xi = \xi_{1:\nvis(t)}$;
sorting makes them prefixes, which the implementation uses everywhere.

The law of $\eta$ is given by \emph{declarations}:
each declaration of variables \code{in Uncertainty(dist)}
gives a group of columns with a joint law (univariate or multivariate),
and distinct declarations are independent.
% Examples of declarations are
%  \code{@variable(m, d in Uncertainty(Uniform(80, 120))} and
%  \code{@variable(m, (d1, d2) in Uncertainty(MvNormal([0, 0], [1 0.5; 0.5 1]))}.
The components of one multivariate declaration may be in different stages;
then the law itself links stages, and we will see in \cref{sec:dual} that its conditional expectations across stages matter for the dual bound.
We write $\law$ for the law of $\eta$, and also of $\xi$.

\subsection{Decisions and the problem}

The decisions are a vector $x \in \R^n$; decision $i$ has a stage $\dstage_i$.
A decision without a stage tag is in the last stage $T = \max(2, \text{largest declared stage})$.
Some decisions may be integer or binary; they must be in stage 1.
\pkg{} accepts a multistage stochastic program with quadratic objective and linear constraints, with uncertain right-hand sides and costs, in the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:SP}
\tag{SP}
\begin{aligned}
\opt \quad & \E\bigl[ x(\xi)^\T P x(\xi) + x(\xi)^\T C \xi + \eta^\T Q \eta + d^\T \eta + f \bigr] \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & A_e x(\xi) = B_e \xi, \quad A_u x(\xi) \le B_u \xi, \quad A_l x(\xi) \ge B_l \xi, \quad \ell \le x(\xi) \le u, \quad \law\text{-a.s.}, \\
& x_i(\xi) \text{ depends only on } \xi_{1:\nvis_i}, \quad x_i(\xi) \in \mathbb{Z} \text{ for the integer decisions},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\opt$ is $\min$ or $\max$, and the numbers $\nvis_i$ of observed columns are defined in \cref{sec:information}.
The data are the sparse matrices
$P \in \R^{n \times n}$ (symmetric: quadratic costs on the decisions),
$C \in \R^{n \times k}$ (random part of the decision costs, by assumption depending linearly on the uncertainties),
$Q \in \R^{m \times m}$ (symmetric),
$d \in \R^m$, $f \in \R$,
$A_e, A_u, A_l$ with $n$ columns, and $B_e, B_u, B_l$ with $k$ columns,
and the bounds $\ell, u \in (\R \cup \{\pm\infty\})^n$.
A constraint \code{lo <= a'x + b'eta <= hi} gives one row in $(A_u, B_u)$ and one in $(A_l, B_l)$,
and an equality one row in $(A_e, B_e)$;
the constant of the right-hand side is the first column of $B$.
Parameters of JuMP are replaced by their values.

The data of \eqref{eq:SP} are those of \citet[(MSP)]{KWG2011} with three extensions:
the objective may be quadratic in the decisions ($P$) and in the uncertainties ($Q$, which only adds a constant to the expected value),
some first-stage decisions may be integer,
and equalities and two-sided rows are kept as such.
Like the papers, the package requires \emph{fixed recourse}:
uncertainties may multiply decisions in the objective (the columns $2, \dots, k$ of $C$),
but not in the constraints, where a product of a decision and an uncertainty is an error (\cref{sec:outlook}).
The constraints of \eqref{eq:SP} hold pointwise;
the expected-value constraints of \citet[(MSP)]{KWG2011} have no counterpart yet.
A row may involve decisions and uncertainties of several stages.
It is a pointwise constraint, required almost surely:
in effect it is checked once all its decisions are taken and all the uncertainties that affect them are revealed,
so its effective stage is the latest stage among its decisions and the uncertainties that appear in it.

\subsection{The information structure}
\label{sec:information}

Decision $i$ observes column $j$ of $\xi$ if $\stage_j \le \dstage_i$, except that integer decisions observe only the constant:
\[
\nvis_i \defeq
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if decision } i \text{ is integer,}\\
\nvis(\dstage_i) = \max\{\, j : \stage_j \le \dstage_i \,\} & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
Since the columns are sorted by stage, the observed columns of every decision form the prefix $1{:}\nvis_i$ (\code{InformationStructure}, \file{src/canonical/information\_structure.jl}).
Integer decisions are therefore here-and-now constants, even when some uncertainties are in stage 1.
% This should probably trigger a warning or error.

For a row $r$ of the constraints, with coefficients $a_r$ on the decisions and $b_r$ on $\xi$, we call
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:structural-columns}
\nvis_r \defeq \max\{\, \nvis_i : a_{ri} \neq 0 \,\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Jr_r \defeq \{1, \dots, \nvis_r\} \cup \{\, j > \nvis_r : b_{rj} \neq 0 \,\}
\end{equation}
the number of columns that its decisions observe and the set of its \emph{structural columns}:
whatever the rules, the function $a_r^\T x(\xi) - b_r^\T \xi$ only involves the columns in $\Jr_r$.
A row is \emph{deterministic} if $\Jr_r = \{1\}$.

\subsection{The support}
\label{sec:support}

The rules will be asked to satisfy the constraints on a set $\Xis$ that contains the support of $\law$.
In the package,
\[
\Xis = \{\, \xi \in \R^k : \xi_1 = 1,\ W \eta - h \in \mathcal{K} \,\},
\]
where $\mathcal{K}$ is the product of a nonnegative orthant, one coordinate per linear row $W_p\eta \ge h_p$,
and of one second-order cone $\mathcal{L}^{q} = \{(t, y) \in \R \times \R^{q-1} : t \ge \norm{y}_2\}$ per conic row of $q$ components.
Without conic rows, $\Xis$ is the polyhedron $\{W\eta \ge h\}$ of \citet{KWG2011};
the conic rows describe sets such as an ellipsoid, and extend both approximations by conic duality \citep[Theorem~2.1]{GWK2015}.
Each of these cones is self-dual, $\mathcal{K}^* = \mathcal{K}$.
The rows come from five sources (\cref{sec:laws}):
the finite bounds of the laws (\code{minimum} and \code{maximum} of each distribution),
the rows that a law adds beyond its bounds (\code{support\_constraints}, such as the ellipsoid of \code{ConfidenceMvNormal}),
constraints over uncertainties only tagged \code{ValidInequality()} (they hold almost surely and only tighten $\Xis$),
constraints tagged \code{Truncate()} (they condition the law, and the conditioned law is supported in them),
both linear or in a second-order cone,
and the convex-hull rows of the liftings (\cref{sec:liftings}).
\citet{KWG2011} assume that $\Xis$ is the support itself, a compact polyhedron;
the package only needs $\supp\law \subseteq \Xis$, and $\Xis$ may be unbounded in some directions,
which costs nothing in the primal approximation and one inequality per row in the dual (\cref{rem:e1-row}).

The rows of $W$ couple some coordinates of $\eta$ and not others.
Let the \emph{support blocks} be the connected components of the graph on the coordinates of $\eta$ in which two coordinates are adjacent when some row involves both (\code{support\_blocks}, \file{src/canonical/support.jl}, by union--find).
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:product-support}
\Xis = \{1\} \times \prod_{b} \Xib{b},
\qquad
\Xib{b} = \{\, \eta_b \in \R^{k_b} : W_b \eta_b - h_b \in \mathcal{K}_b \,\},
\end{equation}
where $\eta_b$ is the subvector of the $k_b$ coordinates of block $b$, $(W_b, h_b)$ the $q_b$ rows of block $b$ restricted to its columns,
and $\mathcal{K}_b$ their cone; a conic row couples its coordinates, so it lies in one block.
When $\mathcal{K}_b$ is a nonnegative orthant, we write $W_b\eta_b \ge h_b$.
All blocks must be bounded, by assumption.
Every block contains the mean of its coordinates:
for the rows of the laws this holds by construction, and the valid rows are checked at the mean when the model is read.
Throughout, we assume the following.

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:support}
$\supp\law \subseteq \Xis$, and the second-moment matrix $\Mom = \E[\xi\xi^\T]$ is finite and positive definite.
\end{assumption}

Positive definiteness of $\Mom$ is equivalent to that of the covariance of $\eta$ (by a Schur complement on the constant column),
that is, to $\eta$ not lying almost surely in a hyperplane.
It implies that the support, hence $\Xis$, spans $\R^k$, which is the condition of \citet[Section~2]{KWG2011};
when $\Xis$ is the support the two conditions are equivalent \citep[Proposition~2]{KWG2011}.
The package rejects equality rows tagged \code{ValidInequality()} but does not check $\Mom \succ 0$;
a law with fewer atoms than its dimension plus one, for instance, violates it.

\subsection{The moments}
\label{sec:moments}

Let $\mub = \E[\xi] = (1, \mu)$ and $\Cv = \Cov(\xi)$, whose first row and column vanish.
Since declarations are independent,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:structured-moments}
\Mom = \E[\xi\xi^\T] = \mub\mub^\T + \Cv,
\qquad
\Cv = \blockdiag_\ell(\Cvb{\ell}),
\end{equation}
with one \emph{covariance block} $\Cvb{\ell}$ per law (\code{Moments}, \file{src/probability/moments.jl}).
The package stores $\mub$ and the blocks, not the dense matrix $\Mom$.

The covariance blocks and the support blocks are two different partitions of the coordinates.
A multivariate law whose support is a box has one covariance block but one support block per coordinate;
a valid inequality that couples two independent declarations makes one support block out of two covariance blocks.
Both partitions are used, and the formulations below do not assume they coincide.
