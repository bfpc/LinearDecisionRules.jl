\section{Computational notes}
\label{sec:computation}

This section collects the sizes of the reformulations and what was measured on two benchmarks:
the $T$-period inventory problem of \file{benchmark/inventory/timing.jl} (one scalar uniform demand per stage, a box support, $T+1$ stages),
and the hydrothermal problem of \citet{ShapiroEtAl2013} in \file{benchmark/hydro} (4 reservoirs, one joint discrete law of 4 inflows per stage, independent across stages).
All times are from HiGHS \citep{HuangfuHall2018} with default options unless stated, on one machine (Windows 11, Julia 1.12.6);
the result files and the analyses are in \file{benchmark/inventory} and \file{benchmark/hydro}, in a folder \file{results} and a \file{README.md} each;
the comparison of solve times with the previous version is summarized in the section ``Past studies'' of \file{benchmark/README.md}.

\subsection{Where the sizes come from}

Each part of the reformulation is attached to one row of the model and grows with what that row sees.
For an inequality $r$ that is not deterministic, with touched support blocks $\Touch_r$ (\cref{sec:primal}):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
& variables & constraints \\
\midrule
rules & $\sum_i \nvis_i$ in total & \\
equality row $r$ & & $\abs{\Jr_r}$ \\
primal, inequality $r$ & $\sum_{b\in\Touch_r} q_b$ & $1 + \sum_{b\in\Touch_r} k_b$ \\
dual, inequality $r$ & $1 + \abs{\Jr_r}$ & $1 + \abs{\Jr_r} + (\text{rows of } \Wp \text{ with a slack term})$ \\
dense primal \citep[(4.2)]{KWG2011} & $2 + \sum_b q_b$ & $1 + k$ \\
dense dual \citep[(4.6)]{KWG2011} & & $1 + \sum_b q_b$, each with up to $k$ slack terms \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In the dense dual each slack term $S_{rj}$ is itself an expression of $X$, and the matrix $\Mom\Wp^\T$ is dense because of the rank-one term $\mub\mub^\T$;
its nonzeros grow as the number of inequalities times the number of rows of $W$ times $k$.
In the structured dual the rank-one term is carried by the single variable $u_r$,
and a row of $\Wp$ gets a slack term only from the structural columns that share a covariance block with its support block.

In a multistage problem the history grows with the stage, so even the structured formulations grow quadratically with $T$ when the stage dimension is fixed:
a stage-$t$ decision has $1 + \sum_{s \le t}(\text{dimension of stage } s)$ coefficients.
For the inventory problem the counts are exact polynomials (\cref{app:sizes}):
the structured primal has $\tfrac{11}{2}T^2 + \tfrac72 T$ variables with multipliers on the boxes ($\tfrac72 T^2 + \tfrac72 T$ with the closed form, the default) and $\tfrac52 T^2 + \tfrac{11}{2}T - 2$ constraints,
the structured dual $\tfrac72T^2 + \tfrac{23}{2}T - 4$ variables and $\tfrac{13}{2}T^2 + \tfrac{19}{2}T - 4$ constraints,
all matching the measured models;
the nonzeros of the dense dual of the previous version grow as $T^3$ (279 thousand, 2.17 million and 17.0 million at $T = 32$, 64 and 128),
those of the structured dual as $T^2$ (17.6, 69.0 and 273 thousand).

The structured primal has 26\% to 33\% of the variables of the legacy one with the closed form on the boxes (41\% to 55\% with multipliers), and the structured dual 13 to 62 times fewer nonzeros;
building the reformulation takes a fraction of a second on all these instances.

When one law couples all stages with a dense covariance, nothing collapses, the primal gain disappears and the dual keeps only the factor of about 2 of masking $S$ by the history.

\subsection{Smaller is not always faster}
\label{sec:faster}

Solve times do not follow the sizes, because presolve does much of the same work (\file{benchmark/README.md}, ``Past studies'').
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Primal.} After presolve, the 0.4 primal with multipliers on the boxes and the legacy primal with \code{SlackAsExpr} are the same linear program
(at hydrothermal $T = 12$, both about 42.2 thousand rows, 150.5 thousand columns and 390.9 thousand nonzeros),
and they take the same time (161 s and 168 s); differences of $\pm30\%$ in iterations come from the order of rows and columns alone.
The smaller formulation saves the time to build and to read the model, and wins clearly without presolve (1.4 s against 6.8 s at inventory $T = 128$).
\item \emph{Dual.} The structured dual is 6 to 150 times faster than the dense one on the inventory problem, from $T = 32$ to $T = 128$ (6.9 s against 1048 s at $T = 128$),
but 4 to 6.5 times slower on the hydrothermal problem (145 s against 22 s at $T = 8$).
There, presolve reduces the dense dual much more (from 200,648 to 33,455 rows, merging pairs of rows into ranges) than the structured one (from 103,280 to 66,743 rows),
and the dual simplex needs four times more iterations.
The cause is the auxiliary $u_r = S_r\mub$: substituting it out gives back the presolved model and the iteration counts of the dense dual on the hydrothermal problem (39 s instead of 145 s at $T = 8$),
but makes the inventory dual 20 times slower (15.2 s instead of 0.72 s at $T = 64$), where the denser rows cost more than the rows saved.
Neither form is better in general, and the package keeps $u_r$.
A density rule choosing per row whether to keep it is not implemented:
the two problems above point in opposite directions, and a threshold on the number of columns of a row would need more instances to be chosen
(\file{benchmark/README.md}, ``Past studies'').
\item \emph{Redundant rows of a lifting.} The support of an uncertainty with $n$ breakpoints needs $n+2$ rows, and the box of its lifted coordinates adds $2n$ implied ones (\cref{sec:axis-lifting}).
Leaving them out saves 30\% to 43\% of the primal variables and 26\% to 37\% of the dual rows, with the same values.
The primal was never measurably slower for it (106 s instead of 187 s on the hydrothermal problem with one breakpoint per inflow at $T = 8$),
and the dual was 8\% to 47\% faster on the inventory problem and on the hydrothermal one at $T = 4$, but 2.6 times slower at $T = 8$ (276 s instead of 105 s), under every seed of HiGHS tried.
So \code{PrimalLDR} leaves them out and \code{DualLDR} keeps them, by default (\code{lifted\_box}; \file{benchmark/README.md}, ``Past studies'').
\item \emph{Solver mode for the bound.} The dual approximation is solved for its value only, and its rule is not a policy, so no basis is needed.
HiGHS's interior-point method without crossover solved the hydrothermal dual at $T = 8$ in 8.0 s instead of 145 s,
with an objective equal to the simplex one to a relative $2\cdot10^{-10}$, but was slower on the inventory dual (1.2 s against 0.72 s).
Each formulation takes raw attributes of its inner solver, for itself only,
such as \code{DualLDR(; attributes = ["solver" => "ipm", "run\_crossover" => "off"])};
none is the default, since the names are those of one solver and the best mode depends on the problem.
A bound taken from an inexact solve is safest when it is the inner solver's dual objective value, which bounds the inner optimum by weak duality;
the package uses it for a linear program solved to optimality and for a solve stopped by a limit with a dual feasible point (\cref{sec:dual}).
Only an \code{OPTIMAL} status can be trusted, though: HiGHS may stop an interior point without crossover at an unknown status,
with a wrong objective value or none, and the package then reports no bound.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Breakpoints and solve time}

Breakpoints multiply the size by about the number of pieces per uncertainty.
On the hydrothermal problem, with $k$ breakpoints per inflow at the equiprobable quantiles of its marginal law (\cref{tab:breakpoints}),
one breakpoint halves the primal value at $T = 4$ and divides it by 2.5 at $T = 8$,
while the dual bound moves by at most 1\%;
the primal problem has 2.6 times more variables and takes 5.2 times (at $T = 4$) to 5.8 times (at $T = 8$) longer to solve.
The out-of-sample means of the primal policies, simulated on 2000 outcomes of the joint law, agree with the primal values within 1.3 standard errors, and no constraint is violated.
The breakpoint variants declare the inflows of a stage as independent marginals (breakpoints apply to univariate declarations);
with no breakpoint this changes nothing in the primal value, the cost being linear, but it splits the covariance blocks, and the dual has 42\% fewer nonzeros (57,296 against 99,344 at $T = 4$).

The gap between the bounds stays large on this problem (the bound is 52\% of the value at $T = 4$ with one breakpoint, 5.3\% at $T = 8$):

\subsection{Building the inner problems}

The reformulations are built from sparse blocks, row by row, as JuMP models;
objectives are assembled term by term with \code{dot}, never as traces of matrix products.
The variables of the inner problems are unnamed by default, since names slow the build down; \code{names = true} gives names such as \code{X[i,j]}, \code{$\lambda$[r,b]}, \code{u[r]}, \code{S[r,j]} for inspection.
The dense formulations of the previous version are kept in \file{test/oracles/dense.jl}, and the tests require that both agree.
