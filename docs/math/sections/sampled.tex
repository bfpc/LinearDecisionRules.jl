\section{Sampled decision rules}
\label{sec:sampled}

The third formulation, \code{SampledLDR(N; seed)} (\file{src/formulations/sampled.jl}), enforces the inequalities on drawn outcomes only.
Draw $\xi^1, \dots, \xi^N$ from $\law$, one independent draw of each declaration per outcome, with a seeded generator,
and solve
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:SLDR}
\tag{S-LDR}
\begin{aligned}
\opt_{X \in \Rules} \quad & \frac1N \sum_{s=1}^N \Bigl( (\xi^s)^\T X^\T P X \xi^s + (\xi^s)^\T X^\T C \xi^s + (\eta^s)^\T Q\eta^s + d^\T\eta^s + f \Bigr) \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & A_e X = B_e, \qquad S_r \xi^s \ge 0, \quad r \in \Rows,\ s = 1, \dots, N.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The objective is the sample average of the objective of \eqref{eq:SP};
since it is quadratic in $\xi$, it is the objective of \cref{prop:objective} with the empirical moments
$\hat\mub = \frac1N\sum_s\xi^s$ and $\hat\Mom = \frac1N\sum_s \xi^s(\xi^s)^\T$,
which the package computes with a single covariance block, the empirical covariance of separate declarations not being zero.

The equalities are matched once, as in \cref{prop:matching}, rather than imposed at every outcome.
Without random recourse, coefficient matching is exact and needs no scenario,
while $N$ copies of the equalities $(A_eX - B_e)\xi^s = 0$ would describe the same affine subspace (when the outcomes span $\R^k$) with many linearly dependent rows,
which leaves interior-point solvers with too few degrees of freedom.

Problem \eqref{eq:SLDR} is the sample average approximation \citep[Chapter~5]{ShapiroDentchevaRuszczynski2014} of the problem already restricted to linear rules, not of \eqref{eq:SP}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item there is one rule $X$ for all outcomes, not one recourse decision per scenario;
\item nonanticipativity is the pattern of $\Rules$, so the outcomes are independent paths and no scenario tree is needed;
\item the constraints hold on the outcomes and nowhere else.
\end{itemize}
Its value is not a bound.
Its constraints are a relaxation of those of \eqref{eq:PLDR}, but its objective is a sample average,
so it is an in-sample estimate, neither above nor below $\val\eqref{eq:PLDR}$ or $\val\eqref{eq:SP}$ in general.
As $N$ grows, one expects it to approach, under the usual regularity conditions, the value of the linear rules feasible on the support of $\law$,
which is $\val\eqref{eq:PLDR}$ when $\Xis$ is the convex hull of the support.
For a given $N$, the scenario approach of \citet{CalafioreCampi2006} bounds the probability that the rule violates some constraint at a new outcome,
in terms of $N$ and the number of coefficients of $X$.
The package reports the rule as a solution of unknown status, and \code{simulate} measures it out of sample:
for outcomes drawn from the laws (or given by the user), it evaluates the rule, the objective at each outcome, its sample mean and standard error,
and the frequency and size of the violations of each constraint (\file{src/jump/simulate.jl}).
For the primal rule, which is feasible on $\Xis$, the same simulation has no violation,
and its sample mean estimates the value of \eqref{eq:PLDR} when the moments are exact.

Random recourse, excluded from the other formulations, would fit \eqref{eq:SLDR} at no cost:
for a fixed outcome, $A(\xi^s)X\xi^s \le B\xi^s$ is linear in $X$ (\cref{sec:outlook}).
