\section{Uncertainty sets and laws}
\label{sec:laws}

The formulations use the law only through $\mub$ and the covariance blocks, and the support only through $\Xis \supseteq \supp\law$.
This section describes where both come from.

\subsection{Roles of the rows of \texorpdfstring{$\Xis$}{the support}}

Each row of $W\eta - h \in \mathcal{K}$ has a role, shared by the components of a conic row (\code{SupportBlock}, \file{src/canonical/support.jl}):
\begin{description}[nosep,font=\normalfont\ttfamily]
\item[:box] a finite bound of a law, from its \code{minimum} and \code{maximum}.
A law without finite bounds gives none; an unbounded law is rejected when it is declared.
\item[:law] a row that the law adds beyond its bounds (\code{support\_constraints}),
linear or in a second-order cone, such as the ellipsoid of \cref{sec:confidence-normal}.
\item[:valid] a constraint over uncertainties only, tagged \code{ValidInequality()}:
it holds almost surely under the declared law and only tightens $\Xis$.
Since it holds on the support, it holds at the mean, which the package checks for a linear row,
unless the row involves a \code{ConditionedDistribution}, whose mean is estimated only later;
equalities are rejected, since $\Xis$ must have a nonempty interior.
\item[:truncate] a constraint tagged \code{Truncate()}:
it \emph{conditions} the law on the set $\mathcal{C}$ where such constraints hold,
and the conditioned law is supported in $\mathcal{C}$, so the row also belongs to $\Xis$.
\item[:hull] a convex-hull row of a lifting (\cref{sec:axis-lifting}).
\end{description}
A constraint over uncertainties only without a tag is an error.
The two tags differ in what they mean for the law, not for $\Xis$:
both give rows of $\Xis$, but only \code{Truncate()} changes $\law$,
hence the moments and the draws.

\paragraph{Conditioning and liftings.}
Rows tagged \code{Truncate()} and breakpoints (\cref{sec:axis-lifting}) are both declarations, so the order in which a model states them does not matter:
the package always conditions first, when it builds the canonical form, and then lifts the conditioned law.
The other order would give the same lifted law:
since $\Lift$ is injective and $\Retr\Lift(\xi) = \xi$, conditioning $\eta$ on $\{W\eta \ge h\}$ and then lifting it
gives the law of $\leta$ conditioned on $\{W\Retr_\eta\leta \ge h\}$.
In the package, a univariate law conditioned by rows on it alone is truncated exactly (\cref{sec:exact-truncation}) and can be lifted;
its breakpoints must lie inside the truncated support, and a number $n$ of breakpoints is spaced over it.
A law merged with others by \code{Truncate()} rows is multivariate, and cannot be lifted yet.
For a law estimated by rejection, the moments of the lifted vector could be estimated from the same draws (\cref{sec:rejection});
this is not done.

\subsection{Exact truncation}
\label{sec:exact-truncation}

When every row tagged \code{Truncate()} that involves a univariate law involves it alone,
and no conic row tagged \code{Truncate()} involves it,
these rows define an interval $[\alpha, \beta]$,
and the law is truncated exactly (\code{\_condition}, \file{src/canonical/conditioning.jl}).
A continuous law becomes \code{truncated(d, alpha, beta)},
whose mean and variance come from Distributions when it has them,
and from quadrature of the truncated density otherwise.
A discrete law with a finite support becomes the law of its atoms in the interval, renormalized.
The rows are then dropped: the box of the truncated law replaces them.
A truncation of probability zero is an error that points to the constraints.

Rows tagged \code{Truncate()} that couple several declarations, or that involve a multivariate law, are handled by merging the declarations they couple, by union--find, into one law:
the product of their laws conditioned on $\mathcal{C} = \{\eta : W_{\mathcal{C}}\eta \ge h_{\mathcal{C}}\}$, which has one covariance block.
When all these laws have finitely many atoms (\code{MvDiscreteNonParametric}, or univariate laws with a finite support), so does their product,
and the conditioned law is exact: the atoms of the product that lie in $\mathcal{C}$, with their probabilities renormalized, an \code{MvDiscreteNonParametric} (\code{\_discrete\_conditioned\_law}).
The rows stay in $\Xis$, and its moments are exact sums, so the values that use them are certified.
The product is enumerated up to $10^6$ atoms; no atom in $\mathcal{C}$ results in an error.

\subsection{Conditioning by rejection sampling}
\label{sec:rejection}

Other conditioned laws have no closed form.
Such a law is a \code{ConditionedDistribution},
whose draws are obtained by rejection sampling:
draws of the product law are kept when they lie in $\mathcal{C}$.

Its moments are those of the empirical law of the kept draws.
Up to $N_{\max}$ draws are kept (\code{RejectionSamplingMaxSamples()}, $10\,000$ by default), within a time limit:
the one of each law when \code{RejectionSamplingTimeLimit()} is set;
otherwise, when the whole solve has a time limit (\code{TimeLimitSec()}, which the sampling counts against), half of the time that remains of it, shared by the laws to sample (each gets an equal share of what the previous ones left), so that the formulations get the rest;
and otherwise 10 seconds per law.
With $n$ kept draws $\eta^1, \dots, \eta^n$ the package uses
\[
\hat\mu = \frac1n\sum_{s=1}^n \eta^s, \qquad \hat\Cv = \frac1n\sum_{s=1}^n (\eta^s - \hat\mu)(\eta^s - \hat\mu)^\T,
\]
dividing by $n$ rather than $n - 1$ (\code{\_estimate}, \file{src/probability/rejection_sampling.jl}).
These are then exactly the moments of a probability law supported in $\mathcal{C}$ and in the boxes of the laws,
the uniform law on the draws:
the mean lies in $\Xis$ and $\hat\Mom$ is a second-moment matrix, so all the statements of \cref{sec:primal,sec:dual} hold for that law.
Sums are accumulated relative to the first kept draw, for accuracy.

The $k$-th conditioned law of a model draws from its own stream,
seeded by the $k$-th entry of a vector of seeds
drawn from the seed of the model (\code{RejectionSamplingSeed()}, default 1234),
so laws never share draws.
Fewer than $\min(N_{\max}, 100)$ kept draws is an error, which also reports the acceptance rate;
the sampling stops early when the estimated upper bound of the acceptance rate
indicates it will not be able to reach that number in time.
Fewer than $N_{\max}$ draws is a warning.
The estimates that reached $N_{\max}$ draws are cached by the conditioned law, its seed and the sample size,
so that a new solve of a model whose laws and conditioning rows did not change
does not sample again; an estimate stopped by the time limit is not cached, so the next solve samples again.
A law conditioned by rejection cannot be lifted yet.

The Monte Carlo error of these moments is of order $n^{-1/2}$:
the standard error of the mean of a coordinate is $\sqrt{\hat\Cv_{jj}/n}$,
one percent of its standard deviation with $10\,000$ draws (the default).
What it means for the results:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item the primal rule is still feasible on all of $\Xis$, since robust feasibility does not involve the moments;
only its value is an estimate of the expected value under the conditioned law;
\item the dual bound is a valid bound for the problem under the empirical law of the draws, and an estimate for the conditioned law;
\item both are reported with \code{certified = false} and a reason appended to the raw status.
\end{itemize}
Values of the dual are also not certified when a law spanning several stages is not known to have linear conditional expectations (\cref{rem:lce}),
and values of the sampled formulation never are, being estimates by construction.

\subsection{Discrete laws}

\code{MvDiscreteNonParametric} is a law on finitely many joint outcomes (scenarios),
with given probabilities.
Its mean and covariance are exact sums,
and random draws are obtained by inversion of the cumulative probabilities.
Currently, the polyhedron $\Xis$ bounding the law
is the box of componentwise minimum and maximum of the atoms;
valid inequalities can describe the hull more tightly,
at the price of coupling the support blocks of each coordinate.
The law has $\Cv \succ 0$ only if its atoms are affinely independent enough, that is, if they span an affine space of full dimension.
In the hydrothermal benchmark (\cref{sec:computation}), each stage has 82 joint outcomes of 4 inflows.

\subsection{The confidence-ellipsoid normal law}
\label{sec:confidence-normal}

A normal law has an unbounded support, and the robust primal approximation would force every rule to ignore it.
\code{ConfidenceMvNormal(mu, Sigma, alpha)} is the normal law $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)$ in $\R^d$ conditioned on its confidence ellipsoid of probability $\alpha$,
\[
\mathcal{E} = \{\, \eta : (\eta - \mu)^\T\Sigma^{-1}(\eta - \mu) \le \rho^2 \,\},
\qquad
\rho^2 = \Fchi{d}^{-1}(\alpha),
\]
where $\Fchi{d}$ is the distribution function of the $\chi^2$ law with $d$ degrees of freedom:
with $\Sigma = LL^\T$ and $z = L^{-1}(\eta - \mu) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$, the ellipsoid is $\norm{z}^2 \le \rho^2$ and $\norm{z}^2 \sim \chi^2_d$.

\begin{proposition}[Moments of \code{ConfidenceMvNormal}]
\label{prop:confidence-moments}
The conditioned law has mean $\mu$ and covariance $c_\alpha\Sigma$, with
\[
c_\alpha = \frac{\Fchi{d+2}(\rho^2)}{\alpha} \in (0, 1).
\]
Moreover, for any split $\eta = (\eta_h, \eta_f)$ of its coordinates,
$\E[\eta_f \mid \eta_h] = \mu_f + \Sigma_{fh}\Sigma_{hh}^{-1}(\eta_h - \mu_h)$, which is linear in $\eta_h$.
\end{proposition}

The proof is in \cref{app:moments}.
The mean is $\mu$ by symmetry, and the covariance is $\Sigma$ scaled by the conditional mean of $\norm{z}^2/d$ on the ball,
which a change of degrees of freedom of the $\chi^2$ density turns into $\Fchi{d+2}(\rho^2)/\alpha$.
The second statement holds because, given $\eta_h$, the ellipsoid is an ellipsoid in $\eta_f$ centered at the conditional mean of the untruncated normal law (a Schur complement),
and the conditional normal law is symmetric around that center.
So a \code{ConfidenceMvNormal} whose components are in several stages satisfies the condition of \cref{thm:dual-bound},
even though its components are correlated and dependent across stages (\cref{rem:lce}).

Draws are by rejection in the standardized space:
draw $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$ until $\norm{z} \le \rho$, and return $\mu + Lz$.
The acceptance probability $\alpha$,
so only a small $\alpha$ is costly, and that is not usually the case.
An exact alternative without rejection draws a direction uniformly on the sphere
and a radius $r$ with $r^2 = \Fchi{d}^{-1}(\alpha U)$, $U$ uniform on $(0,1)$.

\paragraph{Two representations of the support.}
The support $\mathcal{E}$ is not a polyhedron, and the package has two representations of it, the rows of $\Xis$ that the law adds beyond its box
(\code{support\_constraints}, with the role \code{:law}), chosen by the keyword \code{support = :ellipsoid} or \code{:boxes}.
The default, \code{:auto}, takes the ellipsoid when the inner solver supports second-order cones and no decision is integer, and the boxes otherwise;
the dimension of the law does not enter the choice.
\begin{description}[nosep]
\item[Ellipsoid.] The exact set, as the second-order cone row $(\rho,\ L^{-1}(\eta - \mu)) \in \mathcal{L}^{d+1}$,
which makes the support block conic and the formulations second-order cone programs.
Both approximations extend to conic blocks \citep[Theorem~2.1]{GWK2015}:
the multipliers of the row lie in the dual cone (the same cone), and so does $\E[s_r(\xi)\,(\Wp\xi)]$ for the conic rows of $\Wp$.
\item[Boxes.] The bounding box $\abs{\eta_j - \mu_j} \le \rho\sqrt{\Sigma_{jj}}$ (the projection of $\mathcal{E}$ on the axes), the box of the previous version,
and the principal-axis box $\abs{v_j^\T(\eta - \mu)} \le \rho\sqrt{\lambda_j}$, where $\Sigma = V\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)V^\T$:
a polytope with $4d$ rows that contains $\mathcal{E}$, keeping the formulations linear (and mixed-integer linear with integers, much easier than mixed-integer second-order cone programs).
\end{description}
The boxes are an outer approximation, so the primal rule stays feasible and the dual a valid bound; both get looser.
How much, can be computed for a constraint that depends on $\eta$ alone.

\begin{proposition}[Ellipsoid against principal-axis box]
\label{prop:ellipsoid-box}
Let $s(\xi) = s_1 + s_\eta^\T\eta$, and let $\bar s = s_1 + s_\eta^\T\mu = \E[s(\xi)]$ and $\omega = \Lambda^{1/2}V^\T s_\eta$, so that $\norm{\omega}_2 = (s_\eta^\T\Sigma s_\eta)^{1/2}$.
Then
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
\item the primal robust counterpart over $\mathcal{E}$ is $\bar s \ge \rho\norm{\omega}_2$, and over the principal-axis box $\bar s \ge \rho\norm{\omega}_1$;
\item the dual inequalities $\E[s(\xi)(\Wp\xi)] \in \mathcal{L}^{d+1}$ of the ellipsoid row are $\bar s \ge (c_\alpha/\rho)\norm{\omega}_2$,
and those of the principal-axis box are $\bar s \ge (c_\alpha/\rho)\norm{\omega}_\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) With $\eta = \mu + Lz$, $\min_{\norm z \le \rho} s_\eta^\T Lz = -\rho\norm{L^\T s_\eta}_2$ and $\norm{L^\T s_\eta}_2 = \norm{\omega}_2$;
over the box $\{\abs{v_j^\T(\eta - \mu)} \le \rho\sqrt{\lambda_j}\}$, the minimum of $s_\eta^\T(\eta - \mu) = \sum_j (v_j^\T s_\eta)\, v_j^\T(\eta - \mu)$ is $-\rho\sum_j\sqrt{\lambda_j}\abs{v_j^\T s_\eta}$.
(b) $\E[s(\xi)(\eta - \mu)] = c_\alpha\Sigma s_\eta$ by \cref{prop:confidence-moments}.
For the cone row, $\E[s(\xi)(\rho,\ L^{-1}(\eta - \mu))] = (\rho\bar s,\ c_\alpha L^\T s_\eta)$, which lies in $\mathcal{L}^{d+1}$ if and only if $\rho\bar s \ge c_\alpha\norm{\omega}_2$.
For the rows $\pm v_j^\T(\eta - \mu) + \rho\sqrt{\lambda_j} \ge 0$, the inequalities are $\rho\sqrt{\lambda_j}\,\bar s \pm c_\alpha\lambda_j v_j^\T s_\eta \ge 0$, that is, $\rho\bar s \ge c_\alpha\abs{\omega_j}$.
\end{proof}

Since $\norm{\omega}_\infty \le \norm{\omega}_2 \le \norm{\omega}_1 \le \sqrt d\norm{\omega}_2$,
the box representation is at most a factor $\sqrt d$ more conservative in the primal and at most a factor $\sqrt d$ looser in the dual, on these rows;
adding the axis-aligned box tightens both.
The two radii, $\rho$ for the primal and $c_\alpha/\rho$ for the dual, also show how the two approximations see the law:
the primal guards against the worst point of the ellipsoid, the dual only against the spread of the law around its mean.
