\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A multistage stochastic program chooses decisions over time while uncertain data are revealed,
each decision being allowed to depend on what has been observed so far.
Its solution is a policy, a family of functions of the observed history,
and computing an optimal one is hard in general:
the space of functions is infinite-dimensional,
and its discretization by scenario trees grows exponentially with the number of stages.

Decision rules trade optimality for tractability.
Instead of all functions of the history, one looks for policies in a finite-dimensional family,
the simplest being the affine functions of the observed uncertainties \citep{GarstkaWets1974}.
In robust optimization, \citet{BenTal2004} showed that affinely adjustable policies lead to tractable counterparts;
\citet{KWG2011} brought the idea to stochastic programs with expected costs
and, crucially, gave it a dual counterpart.
The \emph{primal} linear decision rule restricts the policies, so its optimal value is attained by a feasible policy
and bounds the optimal value from the side of the objective (an upper bound of a minimization).
The \emph{dual} linear decision rule restricts the multipliers of the constraints,
which amounts to relaxing the constraints to hold in expectation against a finite family of test functions;
its value bounds the optimal value from the other side.
Both are deterministic conic problems, linear programs when the support is a polyhedron,
and the gap between them measures how much was lost by restricting to linear rules.
\citet{GWK2015} extended the construction through \emph{liftings}:
the uncertainty is mapped nonlinearly into a higher-dimensional space,
where linear rules correspond to nonlinear (for instance piecewise affine) rules of the original uncertainty,
and both bounds are obtained by the same machinery on the lifted problem.

\pkg{} automates these constructions for models written in JuMP \citep{JuMP2023}.
The user writes a plain JuMP model, declares the uncertain parameters with their laws
(\code{@variable(model, d in Uncertainty(Uniform(80, 120)))}),
tags decisions and uncertainties with their stage (\code{Stage(t)}),
and may describe the support with additional inequalities,
condition the laws on a set,
and ask for breakpoints of piecewise affine rules.
The package reads the model into a canonical form,
computes the moments and the structure of the support,
builds one deterministic problem per requested formulation,
solves it with the solver chosen by the user,
and reports the value of the primal policy, the dual bound, the rules themselves,
and simulations of the policy on outcomes drawn from the laws or given by the user.

\begin{example}[The newsvendor]
\label{ex:newsvendor}
A newsvendor buys $b \ge 0$ newspapers at price 10 before the demand $d \sim \mathcal{U}(80, 120)$ is revealed,
then sells $s \le d$ at 15 and returns $r$ at 8, with $s + r \le b$; she maximizes her expected profit.
An optimal policy buys $b^\star = 760/7$ and sells $s^\star = \min(b^\star, d)$,
for an expected profit $\Vstar = 3300/7 \approx 471.43$.
The primal linear rule ($b = 120$, $s = d$, $r = 120 - d$) earns $\Vp = 460$,
and the dual bound is $\Vd = 1460/3 \approx 486.67$.
Piecewise affine rules in $d$ (\cref{sec:liftings}) close the gap:
with the breakpoints $\{100\}$, $\{90, 100, 110\}$, and seven and fifteen equally spaced breakpoints,
the primal values are $465$, $471.25$, $471.25$ and $471.33$,
and the dual bounds $476.67$, $474.17$, $471.98$ and $471.51$.
One breakpoint at the critical quantile $760/7$ makes the primal rule optimal.
These values are part of the test suite of the package.
\end{example}

The reformulations of \citet{KWG2011} are compact in matrix notation:
with $x(\xi) = X\xi$, the primal approximation has a matrix of multipliers $\Lambda \ge 0$ with $\Lambda W = S$ and $\Lambda h \ge 0$,
and the dual approximation the constraint $(W - h\eone^\T) \Mom S^\T \ge 0$,
where $S$ is the matrix of the slack rules, $W\xi \ge h$ describes the support and $\Mom$ is the second-moment matrix of $\xi$.
Implemented literally, these matrices are dense, and their sizes grow with the product of the number of constraints and the dimension of the uncertainty.
The previous versions of the package did so, and building the reformulated problem often took longer than solving it;
in multistage problems, the dense dual reached hundreds of millions of nonzeros
(300 million on a 12-stage hydrothermal problem with 3 breakpoints per inflow, 2.6 billion with 24 stages).

The guiding idea of version 0.4 is to keep, throughout, the structure that the model already has:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item the information structure: once the uncertainties are sorted by stage, every decision observes a prefix of them;
\item the support: it is a product of blocks, the connected components of the uncertainties coupled by its inequalities;
\item the moments: $\Mom = \mub\mub^\T + \Cv$ is a rank-one term plus a block-diagonal covariance, one block per independent law;
\item the liftings: their retractions are sparse, and act block by block.
\end{itemize}
Each of these turns a product of dense matrices into a sum over blocks,
so that the part of the reformulation that comes from one constraint grows with what that constraint can see,
not with the whole uncertainty.
On the same hydrothermal problem, the structured dual has 13 to 40 times fewer nonzeros than the dense one, with identical values.

\paragraph{Notation and departures from the papers.}
We follow the notation of \citet{KWG2011} and \citet{GWK2015} where it fits:
$\xi$ with $\xi_1 = 1$, rules $x(\xi) = X\xi$, $\Mom = \E[\xi\xi^\T]$, multipliers $\Lambda$, slack rules $S$, liftings $\Lift$ with retraction $\Retr$.
We depart from it where the package does, and say so where it matters;
the main differences are that the package
allows a quadratic objective and integer first-stage decisions,
treats the set $\Xis$ as a superset of the support of the law described by inequalities (possibly unbounded) rather than as the support itself,
writes the lifted vector $\lxi$ where \citet{GWK2015} write $\xi'$,
and indexes the information structure by prefixes of the columns of $\xi$ rather than by the truncation operators $P_t$ of the papers.
