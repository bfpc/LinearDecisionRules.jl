\section{Outlook}
\label{sec:outlook}

The structured formulations make problems with hundreds of thousands of rule coefficients routine.
For larger ones, the following directions follow from the structure described above; none is implemented,
except for the parts of the paragraphs on solver-aware formulations, conic supports and combinations that are said to be done.

\paragraph{Decomposition.}
The reformulations are block-angular:
the rule coefficients $X$ are shared, and each inequality contributes its own multipliers (primal) or slack variables (dual), linked to $X$ by a few equalities.
This is the structure that Benders and Dantzig--Wolfe decompositions exploit, by inequality, by stage or by support block,
and also the structure of a stage-by-stage scheme in which the rules of the earlier stages are fixed while those of the later ones are optimized.

\paragraph{Column-and-constraint generation.}
Most multipliers of the primal and most cone inequalities of the dual are inactive at the optimum.
For a polyhedral $\Xis$, the robust counterpart of an inequality can be enforced lazily,
by adding the vertices of $\Xis$ that violate it (constraint generation)
or the multipliers of the rows of $W$ that price out (column generation), as in \citet{ZengZhao2013}.
For the dual, cone inequalities can be added as they are violated.

\paragraph{Warm starts across liftings.}
A rule computed with fewer breakpoints is a feasible rule with more (\cref{sec:liftings}),
so the problem without breakpoints, which is the smallest, can warm-start a problem with breakpoints:
its rule gives a primal feasible point, and its active multipliers suggest the columns and constraints to keep.
The same holds along a sequence of nested breakpoints, and could guide where to add them.

\paragraph{Solver-aware formulations.}
Of the two choices measured in \cref{sec:computation}, the attributes of the inner solver per formulation are done
(such as the interior-point method without crossover for the dual, whose value only is needed),
and so are the closed form of the robust counterpart over box blocks and JuMP parameters to re-solve with new data without rebuilding.
A density rule deciding, row by row, whether to keep the auxiliary $u_r$ of the dual is not:
the measurements point in opposite directions on the two benchmarks, and choosing the rule needs more instances.

\paragraph{Conic supports and random recourse.}
Second-order cone rows of $\Xis$, needed for the exact ellipsoid of \cref{sec:confidence-normal}, are done:
they extend both approximations by conic duality \citep[Theorem~2.1]{GWK2015} (\cref{sec:support}).
Random recourse (an uncertainty multiplying a decision in a constraint) makes the robust counterparts quadratic in $\xi$:
\citet[Section~3]{KWG2011} treat it by the S-lemma, exactly for an ellipsoidal support and conservatively for polyhedra.
It is cheap in two cases: when the decision is a first-stage constant, the product is linear in $\xi$;
and in the sampled formulation, where each outcome gives a constraint linear in $X$.

\paragraph{Combinations of uncertainties.}
Breakpoints on combinations are experimental (\cref{sec:combination-lifting}), and three of their limits are open.
The outer approximation of the lifted support is loose with several inputs, since its fibers depend on the uncertainties only through the combination:
a tighter description, between it and the intractable convex hull, would make the pieces more useful.
The moments integrate over every continuous input;
the law of the combination, by convolution, would reduce them to integrals of one or two dimensions.
And a combination over several stages has no dual bound, because condition~\ref{it:lce} of \cref{thm:dual-bound} fails.
One way to restore it is to lift, as coordinates of the earlier stages, the conditional expectations $g_t = \E[p \mid \xi_{1:\nvis(t)}]$ of the pieces given the history,
which are smooth functions with their own hull rows and moments;
for the cumulative sums of $T$ stages this adds $O(T^2)$ coordinates,
so the size grows quickly with the horizon.

\paragraph{Richer rules.}
Nonlinear liftings \citep[Section~5]{GWK2015} fit the framework of \cref{sec:liftings} with new maps $\Lift$ and $\Retr$;
expected-value constraints at a given stage \citep[(MSP)]{KWG2011} fit the canonical form with new rows;
and two-stage linear decision rules \citep{BodurLuedtke2022} combine linear rules with an exact recourse at each stage, at the price of a decomposition.
