\section{The dual approximation}
\label{app:dual}

The key step is that projecting a nonanticipative policy on the linear functions of $\xi$ keeps it nonanticipative.
This is \citet[Lemma~2]{KWG2011}, which we restate with prefixes.

\begin{lemma}[Projection of a function of the history]
\label{lem:projection}
Let $v \le k$, write $\xi = (\xi_h, \xi_f)$ with $\xi_h = \xi_{1:v}$, and suppose that $\E[\xi_f \mid \xi_h] = N_f\xi_h$ almost surely for some matrix $N_f$.
Let $y$ be a square-integrable function of $\xi_h$ with values in $\R^p$, and $Y = \E[y(\xi_h)\xi^\T]\Mom^{-1}$.
Then $Y = (Y_h, 0)$ with $Y_h = \E[y(\xi_h)\xi_h^\T]\Mom_{hh}^{-1}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\Mom_{hh}$ is a principal submatrix of $\Mom \succ 0$, hence invertible, and $Y_h$ is well defined.
Since $\Mom$ is invertible, it suffices to check that $(Y_h, 0)\Mom = \E[y\xi^\T]$.
On the columns $h$, $Y_h\Mom_{hh} = \E[y\xi_h^\T]$ by definition.
On the columns $f$, by the tower property,
$\E[y\xi_f^\T] = \E\bigl[y\,\E[\xi_f\mid\xi_h]^\T\bigr] = \E[y\xi_h^\T]N_f^\T$,
and likewise $\Mom_{hf} = \E[\xi_h\xi_f^\T] = \Mom_{hh}N_f^\T$,
so $Y_h\Mom_{hf} = Y_h\Mom_{hh}N_f^\T = \E[y\xi_h^\T]N_f^\T = \E[y\xi_f^\T]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:dual-bound}]
Let $x$ be a feasible policy of \eqref{eq:SP}: $x_i$ is a square-integrable function of $\xi_{1:\nvis_i}$, integer for the integer decisions, and the constraints hold almost surely.
Let $X = \E[x\xi^\T]\Mom^{-1}$, row by row $X_i = \E[x_i\xi^\T]\Mom^{-1}$.

\emph{Information structure.}
For a decision of stage $t$ with $\nvis_i = \nvis(t)$, condition~\ref{it:lce} gives $\E[\xi_f\mid\xi_{1:\nvis_i}] = N_f\xi_{1:\nvis_i}$ for the trailing columns $f$,
and \cref{lem:projection} shows that $X_{ij} = 0$ for $j > \nvis_i$.
For an integer decision, $\nvis_i = 1$, $x_i$ is a constant $c_i \in \mathbb{Z}$, and $X_i = c_i\E[\xi^\T]\Mom^{-1} = c_i\eone^\T$ because $\Mom\eone = \mub$;
so $X_{i1} = c_i$ is integer, and the same argument gives $X_{i1} = x_i$ for every decision with $\nvis_i = 1$, which therefore satisfies its bounds.
Hence $X \in \Rules$.

\emph{Equalities.}
$A_ex(\xi) = B_e\xi$ almost surely; multiplying by $\xi^\T$ and taking expectations, $A_eX\Mom = B_e\Mom$, so $A_eX = B_e$.

\emph{Inequalities.}
For $r \in \Rows$, let $s_r(\xi) = \sigma_r(b_r^\T\xi - a_r^\T x(\xi)) \ge 0$ almost surely.
By linearity, $\E[s_r(\xi)\xi^\T] = \sigma_r(b_r^\T\Mom - a_r^\T X\Mom) = S_r\Mom$ with $S_r = \sigma_r(b_r^\T - a_r^\T X)$.
Since $\supp\law \subseteq \Xis$, $\Wp\xi \ge 0$ almost surely, so $s_r(\xi)(\Wp\xi)^\T \ge 0$ almost surely, and taking expectations,
$S_r\Mom\Wp^\T = \E[s_r(\xi)(\Wp\xi)^\T] \ge 0$.
For a deterministic inequality, all its decisions observe only the constant, so $s_r$ is the constant $S_{r1}$, which is nonnegative.

\emph{Objective.}
The terms in $\eta$ alone do not depend on the policy.
The linear term is $\E[x^\T C\xi] = \tr(C^\T\E[x\xi^\T]) = \tr(C^\T X\Mom) = \E[(X\xi)^\T C\xi]$.
For the quadratic term, let $\varepsilon = x - X\xi$, so that $\E[\varepsilon\xi^\T] = 0$.
Then $\E[\varepsilon^\T P X\xi] = \tr(PX\E[\xi\varepsilon^\T]) = 0$, and
\[
\E[x^\T Px] = \E[(X\xi)^\T P(X\xi)] + \E[\varepsilon^\T P\varepsilon],
\]
where the last term is nonnegative if $P \succeq 0$ and nonpositive if $P \preceq 0$.
So, for a minimization with $P \succeq 0$, the objective of $X$ in \eqref{eq:DLDR} is at most that of $x$, and symmetrically for a maximization.

Taking the infimum over feasible policies gives $\val\eqref{eq:DLDR} \le \val\eqref{eq:SP}$ for a minimization;
$\val\eqref{eq:SP} \le \val\eqref{eq:PLDR}$ holds because a feasible rule of \eqref{eq:PLDR} is a feasible policy of \eqref{eq:SP}, by $\supp\law\subseteq\Xis$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proof uses condition~\ref{it:lce} only through \cref{lem:projection}, at the prefixes $\nvis_i$ of the decisions.
It does not need the strict feasibility of \citet[Proposition~4]{KWG2011}, which serves to show that \eqref{eq:DLDR} has the same value as their relaxed problem (MSP$^l$), not that it bounds \eqref{eq:SP}.
The equalities, the inequalities and the objective hold for $X = \E[x\xi^\T]\Mom^{-1}$ whether or not $x$ is nonanticipative,
and so does the step for the decisions with $\nvis_i = 1$, which are constants;
a deterministic inequality has only such decisions.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:relaxed-mask}]
By the remark, it suffices to show that $X \in \Rules'$.
Fix a decision $i$ of stage $t$ with $\nvis_i > 1$, its history $h = \{1, \dots, \nvis_i\}$, and a law $\ell$ with earlier columns $p$ and later columns $f$ at $t$.
Let $\hat\xi_f = \mub_f + \Cv_{fp}\Cv_{pp}^{-1}(\xi_p - \mub_p)$ be the best affine predictor of $\xi_f$ from $\xi_p$, and $e = \xi_f - \hat\xi_f$ its residual,
which is orthogonal to $1$ and to $\xi_p$, with $\E[ee^\T] = R_{\ell,t}$.
For $j \in f$, let $v$ be the vector with $v^\T\xi = e_j$, and compute $\Mom v = \E[\xi e_j]$ entry by entry:
for $m = 1$ and $m \in p$ the entry is $0$ by orthogonality;
for a column $m$ of another law it is $\E[\xi_m]\E[e_j] = 0$, by independence;
for $m \in f$ it is $\E[\xi_m e_j] = \E[e_m e_j] = (R_{\ell,t})_{mj}$, since $\hat\xi_m$ is orthogonal to $e_j$.
Hence $X_i\Mom v = X_{i,f}R_{\ell,t}\,e_j$, and on the other hand $X_i\Mom v = \E[x_i\xi^\T]v = \E[x_i e_j]$.
If $j$ is predictable, $\E[\xi_j\mid\xi_p] = \hat\xi_j$ (an affine conditional expectation is the best affine predictor),
and since $(\xi_p, \xi_j)$ is independent of the columns of the other laws, $\E[\xi_j\mid\xi_h] = \E[\xi_j\mid\xi_p]$, so $\E[e_j\mid\xi_h] = 0$.
As $x_i$ is a square-integrable function of $\xi_h$, $\E[x_ie_j] = \E[x_i\E[e_j\mid\xi_h]] = 0$, which is the equation $X_{i,f}R_{\ell,t}e_j = 0$.
If every $j \in f$ is predictable, these equations give $X_{i,f}R_{\ell,t} = 0$, hence $X_{i,f} = 0$, since $R_{\ell,t}$, a Schur complement of $\Cv_\ell \succ 0$, is invertible.
Every column after $\nvis_i$ belongs to exactly one law, so $X \in \Rules'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:relaxed-lifting}]
Write the lifted outcome $\tilde\xi = (\xi, \pi)$, so that $\xi = Q\tilde\xi$ with $Q = [I\ 0]$, the lifted data $B Q$, $CQ$, $\Wp Q$, and $\Mom = Q\tilde\Mom Q^\T$.
The upper bound: a feasible policy $x$ of \eqref{eq:SP} is a feasible policy of the lifted problem, $x(Q\tilde\xi)$, nonanticipative since the lifted coordinates of a stage are functions of the history; apply \cref{prop:relaxed-mask} to the lifted problem.
The lower bound: let $\tilde X$ be feasible in the relaxed dual of the lifted problem, $\tilde Y = \tilde X\tilde\Mom$, and $X = \tilde Y Q^\T\Mom^{-1}$, so that $X\Mom = \tilde X\tilde\Mom Q^\T$.
By condition~\ref{it:lce} for \eqref{eq:SP}, every column of $\xi$ after the history of a decision is predictable in the lifted problem too (its conditional expectation given the history is affine in columns of $\xi$, which are lifted columns), so the relaxed dual imposes its equation, $\E[\tilde x_i e_j] = 0$ written in $\tilde Y$; in $Y = X\Mom$ these are the equations of the mask of \eqref{eq:SP}, and with $\Mom \succ 0$ they give $X \in \Rules$.
The equalities: $A_e\tilde X = B_eQ$ gives $A_eX\Mom = A_e\tilde X\tilde\Mom Q^\T = B_eQ\tilde\Mom Q^\T = B_e\Mom$.
The cones: $S_r\Mom = \tilde S_r\tilde\Mom Q^\T$, so $S_r\Mom\Wp^\T = \tilde S_r\tilde\Mom(\Wp Q)^\T$, a constraint of the lifted dual.
The objective: the linear term is $\tr(C^\T X\Mom) = \tr((CQ)^\T\tilde X\tilde\Mom)$, and for $P \succeq 0$,
$\tr(X^\T PX\Mom) = \tr(P\tilde Y Q^\T(Q\tilde\Mom Q^\T)^{-1}Q\tilde Y^\T) \le \tr(P\tilde Y\tilde\Mom^{-1}\tilde Y^\T) = \tr(\tilde X^\T P\tilde X\tilde\Mom)$,
since $\tilde\Mom^{1/2}Q^\T(Q\tilde\Mom Q^\T)^{-1}Q\tilde\Mom^{1/2}$ is an orthogonal projector.
So $X$ is feasible in \eqref{eq:DLDR} for \eqref{eq:SP}, with an objective no worse.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The structured identity, row by row.}
Index the rows of $\Wp$ by the constant row and by the pairs $(b, p)$ of a support block and one of its rows, and let $\Cv_{:,b}$ be the columns of $\Cv$ of the coordinates of block $b$.
Since the first row and column of $\Cv$ vanish and $\eone^\T\mub = 1$,
\[
S_r\Mom\eone = S_r\mub = u_r,
\qquad
S_r\Mom\Wp_{bp}{}^\T = u_r\,(\Wp_{bp}\mub) + S_r\Cv\Wp_{bp}{}^\T = u_r\,(W_{bp}\mu_b - h_{bp}) + S_r\Cv_{:,b}W_{bp}^\T,
\]
which are the inequalities $u_r \ge 0$ and \eqref{eq:dual-cone-row}.
The column $j$ of $\Cv_{:,b}$ is nonzero only if coordinate $j$ is in a covariance block that meets support block $b$,
so the term $S_r\Cv_{:,b}W_{bp}^\T = \sum_{j\in\Jr_r} S_{rj}(V_b)_{jp}$ has at most $\abs{\Jr_r}$ terms, and none when no structural column of $r$ shares a covariance block with $b$.

\subsection{Further results on the information structure}
\label{app:information}

These results complete \cref{prop:relaxed-mask}.
The package uses none of them yet; they say how far the relaxation could be tightened, when the mask is valid again, and what a singular $\Mom$ would need.
The laws $\ell$, their columns, and the sets $p$ and $f$ of earlier and later columns at a stage $t$ are as in \cref{sec:dual};
the laws are independent, and $\mathcal F_t$ denotes the information of the history $\xi_{1:\nvis(t)}$.

\paragraph{The exact image of nonanticipativity.}
A \emph{predictable direction} of law $\ell$ at $t$ is a vector $c \in \R^f$ such that $\E[c^\T\xi_f \mid \xi_p]$ is affine in $\xi_p$;
they form a subspace $\mathcal C_{\ell,t}$, which contains $e_j$ for every predictable column $j$.
With $\hat\xi_f$ the best affine predictor of $\xi_f$ from $\xi_p$, as in the proof of \cref{prop:relaxed-mask}, let $v_c$ be the vector with $v_c^\T\xi = c^\T(\xi_f - \hat\xi_f)$.

\begin{proposition}[Projections of the functions of the history]
\label{prop:exact-image}
Under \cref{ass:support}, the rows $\E[g\,\xi^\T]$, for $g$ a square-integrable function of the history at $t$, are exactly the $y \in \R^{1\times k}$ with $y\,v_c = 0$ for every law $\ell$ and every $c \in \mathcal C_{\ell,t}$.
In terms of $X_i = y\Mom^{-1}$, these equations are $X_{i,f}R_{\ell,t}\,c = 0$, on the later columns of each law.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal Y$ be the set of the rows $\E[g\xi^\T]$, a subspace, and for $v \in \R^k$ let $\phi_v = \E[v^\T\xi \mid \mathcal F_t]$.
By the tower property, $\E[g\xi^\T]v = \E[g\,\phi_v]$; taking $g = \phi_v$ shows that $v \perp \mathcal Y$ if and only if $\phi_v = 0$ almost surely.
In finite dimension, $\mathcal Y$ is then the orthogonal of $\mathcal V = \{v : \phi_v = 0 \text{ a.s.}\}$, and it remains to show that $\mathcal V$ is spanned by the vectors $v_c$.
Each $v_c$ is in $\mathcal V$: by independence $\E[\xi_f \mid \mathcal F_t] = \E[\xi_f \mid \xi_p]$, and an affine conditional expectation is the best affine predictor, so $\phi_{v_c} = c^\T(\E[\xi_f\mid\xi_p] - \hat\xi_f) = 0$.
Conversely, write $v^\T\xi = a_0 + \sum_\ell (a_\ell^\T\xi_{p_\ell} + c_\ell^\T\xi_{f_\ell})$ and suppose $\phi_v = a_0 + \sum_\ell(a_\ell^\T\xi_{p_\ell} + c_\ell^\T\E[\xi_{f_\ell}\mid\xi_{p_\ell}]) = 0$.
Conditioning on $\xi_{p_\ell}$, the terms of the other laws become constants, so $c_\ell^\T\E[\xi_{f_\ell}\mid\xi_{p_\ell}]$ is affine: $c_\ell \in \mathcal C_{\ell,t}$, and that term is $c_\ell^\T\hat\xi_{f_\ell}$.
Then $v^\T\xi = v^\T\xi - \phi_v = \sum_\ell v_{c_\ell}^\T\xi$, and since $\Mom \succ 0$ the components of $\xi$ are linearly independent, so $v = \sum_\ell v_{c_\ell}$.
Finally, the computation of $\Mom v$ in the proof of \cref{prop:relaxed-mask}, with $c$ in place of $e_j$, gives $X_i\Mom v_c = X_{i,f}R_{\ell,t}\,c$.
\end{proof}

So the equations of \cref{prop:relaxed-mask} are those of the coordinate directions of $\mathcal C_{\ell,t}$.
Imposing all of $\mathcal C_{\ell,t}$ gives the smallest set of rules, defined row by row, that contains the projections of every nonanticipative policy;
it ignores the constraints of the problem, which could restrict the projections further, through several rows at once.
A direction adds strength when, say, two later columns are not predictable but their sum is.
For an \code{MvDiscreteNonParametric} law, $c \in \mathcal C_{\ell,t}$ when the vector of the conditional means $\E[c^\T\xi_f \mid \xi_p]$, one per distinct history, is in the range of the matrix $[\one\ \ \text{histories}]$,
whatever the probabilities: $\mathcal C_{\ell,t}$ is a kernel, computed up to a tolerance, and a direction wrongly accepted would make the bound invalid.
Under the rescaling of \cref{sec:rescaling}, a predictable column stays predictable, and a direction $c$ becomes $Sc$.

\begin{corollary}[When the mask of a law is valid]
\label{cor:mask-valid}
Under \cref{ass:support}, $X_{i,f} = 0$ for the projection $X_i = \E[x_i\xi^\T]\Mom^{-1}$ of every square-integrable function $x_i$ of the history at $t$
if and only if every later column of law $\ell$ is predictable at $t$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If every later column is predictable, $\mathcal C_{\ell,t} = \R^f$, and the equations give $X_{i,f}R_{\ell,t} = 0$, hence $X_{i,f} = 0$.
Conversely, let $j \in f$ be a column that is not predictable, $g = \E[\xi_j \mid \xi_p]$, a function of the history, and $x_i = g$.
With $e_j = \xi_j - \hat\xi_j$, the tower property and the orthogonality of $e_j$ to the affine functions of $\xi_p$ give
$\E[g\,e_j] = \E[g(g - \hat\xi_j)] = \E[(g - \hat\xi_j)^2] + \E[\hat\xi_j(\xi_j - \hat\xi_j)] = \E[(g - \hat\xi_j)^2] > 0$.
As in the proof of \cref{prop:relaxed-mask}, $\E[g\,e_j] = X_{i,f}R_{\ell,t}\,e_j$, so $X_{i,f} \ne 0$.
\end{proof}

So when a law fails condition~\ref{it:lce}, some function of the history projects outside the mask;
whether the masked dual of a given problem is wrong then depends on the problem, and the example after \cref{prop:relaxed-mask} is one where it is.
The relaxation is local to the laws because they are independent; uncorrelated is not enough.
With $\eta_2$ uniform on $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ in stage 2 and $\eta_3 = \eta_2^2 - \tfrac23 + \epsilon$ in stage 3, $\epsilon$ independent and centered, taken as two laws,
$\Mom$ is block diagonal, each law is in one stage, but $\E[\eta_3 \mid \eta_2] = \eta_2^2 - \tfrac23$,
and the projection of the decision $x = \eta_2^2$ of stage 2 has the coefficient $\tfrac29/(\tfrac29 + \E\epsilon^2) \ne 0$ on $\eta_3$.
The declarations of the package are independent by construction (\cref{rem:lce}).

\paragraph{Cumulative sums.}
Let $\eta_2, \dots, \eta_T$ be independent scalars, $\eta_t$ in stage $t$, and $s_t = \eta_2 + \dots + \eta_t$ with breakpoints $B_t$ interior to the range $(s_t^{\min}, s_t^{\max})$ of $s_t$, lifted by the combination lifting of \cref{sec:combination-lifting} in stage $t$.
The pieces of $s_t$ span, with $1$ and $s_t$, the space $\mathrm{PL}(s_t; B_t)$ of the continuous piecewise linear functions of $s_t$ with kinks in $B_t$, spanned by $1$, $s_t$ and the hinges $(s_t - b)_+$, $b \in B_t$.
The pieces are functions of the inputs, so the information of a stage is that of $\eta_2, \dots, \eta_t$, and the later inputs have constant conditional expectations.
So condition~\ref{it:lce} holds at stage $\tau$ if and only if, for every $t > \tau$ and $b \in B_t$, the function
\[
\psi_{\tau,t,b}(s_\tau) = \E[(s_t - b)_+ \mid \mathcal F_\tau] = \E\bigl[(\sigma + D - b)_+\bigr]_{\sigma = s_\tau},
\qquad D = \eta_{\tau+1} + \dots + \eta_t,
\]
is almost surely in $\mathcal L_\tau$, the span of $1$, $\eta_2, \dots, \eta_\tau$ and the pieces of $s_2, \dots, s_\tau$.

\begin{proposition}[Continuous inputs]
\label{prop:continuous-sums}
Suppose that each $\eta_u$ has a density, positive on an open interval and zero outside.
Then for every $2 \le \tau < t$ and $b \in B_t$, $\psi_{\tau,t,b}(s_\tau) \notin \mathcal L_\tau$, whatever the finite breakpoints $B_2, \dots, B_\tau$:
the last piece of $s_t$ is not predictable at $\tau$, and condition~\ref{it:lce} fails at every stage $\tau \ge 2$ before the last stage $t$ with $B_t \neq \emptyset$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
First, a continuous function $\phi(s_\tau)$ in $\mathcal L_\tau$ is in $\mathrm{PL}(s_\tau; B_\tau)$ on $(s_\tau^{\min}, s_\tau^{\max})$.
Indeed, in the coordinates $(s_2, \dots, s_\tau)$, a linear bijection of $(\eta_2, \dots, \eta_\tau)$, the law has a density positive on a connected open set $O$,
and an almost sure identity between continuous functions holds on all of $O$.
Writing the linear part of $\mathcal L_\tau$ in these coordinates, the identity reads $\sum_{u<\tau}h_u(s_u) + h_\tau(s_\tau) = 0$ on $O$, with $h_u \in \mathrm{PL}(s_u; B_u)$ for $u < \tau$ and $h_\tau = g - \phi$ for some $g \in \mathrm{PL}(s_\tau; B_\tau)$.
Near any point of $O$ each coordinate can move alone, so each $h_u$ is locally constant on the projection of $O$, an interval, hence constant: $\phi \in \mathrm{PL}(s_\tau; B_\tau)$, and the breakpoints of the earlier sums do not help.
Second, $\psi(\sigma) = \E[(\sigma + D - b)_+]$ is continuously differentiable, with $\psi'(\sigma) = \Prob(D > b - \sigma)$, continuous because $D$ has a density.
A continuously differentiable function that is continuous piecewise linear on an interval is affine there.
Third, $D$ has a density positive on the interval $(d^{\min}, d^{\max})$ of the sums of the bounds of its terms, so $\psi'$ is strictly monotone where $b - \sigma$ lies in it;
since $b$ is interior to the range of $s_t$, that happens for $\sigma$ in an open subinterval of $(s_\tau^{\min}, s_\tau^{\max})$, where $\psi$ is not affine.
By the first two steps, $\psi(s_\tau) \in \mathcal L_\tau$ would make $\psi$ affine on all of $(s_\tau^{\min}, s_\tau^{\max})$, so $\psi(s_\tau) \notin \mathcal L_\tau$.
\end{proof}

The conditional expectation of a later hinge is a smoothed hinge, which no piecewise linear function of the history matches.
With discrete inputs, the smoothed hinge is again a hinge, at shifted points, and breakpoints that contain these points restore the condition.

\begin{proposition}[Breakpoints closed under the shifts]
\label{prop:shift-closed}
Suppose that $\eta_3, \dots, \eta_T$ have finite supports, $\eta_2$ being arbitrary, and that for every $\tau = 2, \dots, T - 1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shift-closed}
B_\tau \supseteq \bigl(B_{\tau+1} - \supp\eta_{\tau+1}\bigr) \cap (s_\tau^{\min}, s_\tau^{\max}).
\end{equation}
Then condition~\ref{it:lce} holds at every stage for the lifted law, and \cref{thm:dual-bound} applies to the lifted problem when its other assumptions hold.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We show that $\E[(s_t - b)_+ \mid \mathcal F_\tau] \in \mathrm{PL}(s_\tau; B_\tau)$ almost surely for $t > \tau$ and $b \in B_t$, by induction on $t - \tau$.
For $t = \tau + 1$, by independence, $\E[(s_\tau + \eta_{\tau+1} - b)_+ \mid \mathcal F_\tau] = \sum_q \pi_q\,(s_\tau - (b - d_q))_+$ over the atoms $d_q$ of $\eta_{\tau+1}$, of probabilities $\pi_q$;
a hinge at $c = b - d_q$ is in $\mathrm{PL}(s_\tau; B_\tau)$ by \eqref{eq:shift-closed} if $c$ is interior to the range of $s_\tau$, almost surely equal to the affine $s_\tau - c$ if $c \le s_\tau^{\min}$, and to $0$ if $c \ge s_\tau^{\max}$.
For $t > \tau + 1$, $\E[(s_t - b)_+ \mid \mathcal F_\tau] = \E\bigl[\E[(s_t - b)_+ \mid \mathcal F_{t-1}] \bigm| \mathcal F_\tau\bigr]$;
the inner one is in $\mathrm{PL}(s_{t-1}; B_{t-1})$ by the first case, $\E[s_{t-1} \mid \mathcal F_\tau]$ is $s_\tau$ plus a constant, and each hinge of $s_{t-1}$ at $B_{t-1}$ falls under the induction hypothesis.
\end{proof}

Condition~\eqref{eq:shift-closed} involves only consecutive stages, and propagates backward from $B_T$.
It is sufficient; it is also necessary at $\tau = 2$ when $\eta_2$ has a density, since $\mathcal L_2 = \mathrm{PL}(s_2; B_2)$ and the kinks $b - d_q$ in the range of $s_2$ have slope jumps $\pi_q > 0$.
For $\tau \ge 3$, whose history has atoms, the exact condition is open.
Its sets can grow like $\abs{B_T}\prod_u\abs{\supp\eta_u}$ when the supports are not on a lattice.
When $s_\tau$ is itself discrete, with atoms $\sigma_0 < \dots < \sigma_m$, only the values on the atoms matter:
a kink strictly between two consecutive atoms, $c = \lambda\sigma_a + (1 - \lambda)\sigma_{a+1}$, agrees on the atoms with $\lambda(s - \sigma_a)_+ + (1 - \lambda)(s - \sigma_{a+1})_+$, and a hinge at an extreme atom is affine there.
So each point of \eqref{eq:shift-closed} can be replaced by itself if it is an atom of $s_\tau$, and by its two neighbouring atoms otherwise, dropping the extreme atoms.
This pruned closure is still sufficient, and keeps $B_\tau$ within the interior atoms of $s_\tau$, whose number can still grow exponentially with $\tau$.
With all of them as breakpoints, every function of $s_\tau$ is in $\mathrm{PL}(s_\tau; B_\tau)$ on the atoms, which restores the condition at $\tau$ for any later inputs,
and the covariance of the lifting of $s_\tau$ alone is nonsingular:
a function of $\mathrm{PL}(s_\tau; B_\tau)$ that vanishes at every atom is affine with two zeros between consecutive atoms, and beyond the extreme breakpoints, hence zero;
whereas the unpruned closure can put two breakpoints between consecutive atoms and make it singular.
Whether the covariance of the whole lifted law is then nonsingular is open; the package would check it, as for any law.
In the example after \cref{prop:relaxed-mask}, the pruned closure gives $B_2 = \{0\}$, which restores the condition, and makes the primal approximation exact, since $\abs{\eta_2} = -\eta_2 + 2(\eta_2)_+$.

\paragraph{A singular second-moment matrix.}
The proof of \cref{thm:dual-bound} inverts $\Mom$ in two places: the definition of $X$, and the step from $A_eX\Mom = B_e\Mom$ to $A_eX = B_e$.
Only the second one is a real need.

\begin{proposition}[Without $\Mom \succ 0$]
\label{prop:singular-moments}
Suppose $\supp\law \subseteq \Xis$, $\Mom$ finite, and condition~\ref{it:convex}, but not necessarily $\Mom \succ 0$.
In \eqref{eq:DLDR}, replace the equalities by $(A_eX - B_e)\Mom = 0$, and the rules $X \in \Rules$ by the equations $X_i\Mom v = 0$, for each decision $i$ with $\nvis_i > 1$ and each $v$ with $\E[v^\T\xi \mid \mathcal F_{t_i}] = 0$ almost surely, keeping $X_i = c_i\eone^\T$ for the decisions with $\nvis_i = 1$.
Then for every feasible policy $x$ of \eqref{eq:SP}, there is a feasible $X$ with $X\Mom = \E[x\xi^\T]$, whose objective is at least as good.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $Y = \E[x\xi^\T]$.
If $\Mom v = 0$, then $\E[(v^\T\xi)^2] = 0$, so $v^\T\xi = 0$ almost surely and $Yv = \E[x\,v^\T\xi] = 0$: the rows of $Y$ are in the range of $\Mom$, and $X = Y\Mom^+$ has $X\Mom = Y$.
For a decision with $\nvis_i = 1$, $Y_i = c_i\mub^\T = c_i\eone^\T\Mom$, so the row $c_i\eone^\T$ may be taken instead.
The equalities, the inequalities and the objective of the proof of \cref{thm:dual-bound} depend on $X$ only through $X\Mom$, once the equalities are left as $(A_eX - B_e)\Mom = 0$:
the quadratic term too, since $X\Mom X^\T = X\Mom\Mom^+\Mom X^\T = Y\Mom^+Y^\T$;
and $\varepsilon = x - X\xi$ still has $\E[\varepsilon\xi^\T] = Y - X\Mom = 0$.
The equations of the rules hold by the first part of the proof of \cref{prop:exact-image}, which does not use $\Mom$.
\end{proof}

Coefficient matching is the step that fails: with $\eta_3 = \eta_2$ almost surely, both in stage 2, and the equalities $x = \eta_2$ and $x = \eta_3$ on a decision of stage 2,
\eqref{eq:SP} is feasible, but $X_i = e_2^\T$ and $X_i = e_3^\T$ are incompatible, while $(e_2 - e_3)^\T\Mom = 0$.
With $Z = A_eX - B_e$, the equation $Z\Mom = 0$ splits by blocks: since $Z\Mom Z^\T = (Z\mub)(Z\mub)^\T + Z\Cv Z^\T$ is a sum of two positive semidefinite terms,
it is equivalent to $Z\mub = 0$ and $Z_{J_\ell}\Cv_\ell = 0$ for every law $\ell$.
The package checks $\Mom \succ 0$ on each covariance block and does not certify the bound otherwise (after \cref{ass:support});
with the equalities written this way, it could.
